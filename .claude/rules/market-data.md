# Market data, the latest price and the calendar

The board's quotes, the price line a story is given, and the economic calendar. Dated entries; where two differ, the later one stands.

- Market data: `pipeline/markets.mjs` writes `src/data/markets.json` (delayed quotes from Yahoo Finance's public chart endpoint, no key; open.er-api.com as the currency fallback; an instrument that fails keeps its last quote marked stale, and the script never fails a build). The deploy workflow refreshes it before every build and rebuilds every two hours on its own; the newsroom commits it as the fallback. `src/lib/markets.ts` formats it; `MarketStrip.astro` runs under the section bar on every page, `MarketBoard.astro` is the compact quote table (one `group` per market group on the data page; the `box` beside the cover went on 2026-10-19), `QuoteCard.astro` a card with a month's sparkline. The data lives on its own page `/markets/data/` (`src/pages/markets/data.astro`: headline cards, five group tables in ruled columns, the latest market news); the الأسواق section itself opens with news (its six-card الأسواق في لمحة band went on 2026-10-19: the strip is the site's one price line, the owner's choice; a page that prints an instrument itself, a subject's prices or the board, leaves it out of the strip through Base.astro's `quotesShown`, and the board's page runs none). Never open a section with tables. Never hand-edit the JSON. The rise/fall colours `--up`/`--down` are the one functional colour pair on the site and appear nowhere else.
- The economic calendar: `pipeline/calendar.mjs` writes `src/data/calendar.json` from the institutions' own schedule pages (Fed FOMC dates, ECB monetary policy meetings, BoE MPC dates, BLS CPI and jobs releases); never from memory, never hand-edited; a source that fails keeps its stored events. `src/lib/calendar.ts` reads it; `Agenda.astro` is the front's module (third column) and `/calendar/` the page. Refreshed with the quotes before every build.
- The latest price (the owner, 2026-10-19, "build 1 and 2", after a same-event benchmark against الشرق بلومبرغ, Reuters and the FT found our oil story printing a WTI price six hours old): `pipeline/lib/context.mjs` `marketData()` reads the delayed quotes of the instruments a news story is about (named in its headline, or in its reports' titles for a markets or energy story; Brent always with WTI and first; a yield story only when the headline names the yield) just before the desk notes, as one source (`kind: "market"`, filed under its Yahoo Finance quote page) carrying each price's time and move. The writer reports the later of its price and the reports'; it never counts as an outlet; the second look and the private pairs never fetch it again (`MARKET_QUOTE_URL` in `lib/quotes.mjs`, which now holds the quote code `pipeline/markets.mjs` uses), reading it only from the copy saved at publication, since a later price would read as a contradiction. Item 1, handing the writer our own earlier stories on the same subject, was built and withdrawn the same day, before it shipped, after the owner asked whether old stories would make the writer hallucinate: no matching method tried (headline words, tag words, whole subject tags) was right more than about a third of the time, and the case that prompted it (Anthropic's profitable quarter) was never in our archive while a contrary older fact was. The measurements are in the header of `lib/context.mjs`.

- The board's moves, 2026-11-09 (the owner: "check for bugs and fix them at the source. for example market boards, currencies exchange rates"). The live board was wrong three ways, all from Yahoo's one-day range, whose `chartPreviousClose` had been every move's base: between a market's new day and its first trade that range is the new, empty day and its previous close is the quote's own close, so every commodity read «دون تغيير 0.00%» on Sunday evening (Brent's +0.03% and gold's −0.94% of Friday), wheat all night, and the Nikkei for twenty minutes after Tokyo opened beside its 5.97% fall; the EGX30's base was a session stale again (up 0.32% printed where it had lost 0.67%); and natural gas's answer gave a time of 15 October and a base of 3.533 beside Friday's price, printed as −15.14% dated 15 October. `yahooQuote()` (lib/quotes.mjs, shared with the newsroom's market lines) now measures the move from the close of the session before the quote's own, read from the three months of daily closes (`prevFrom: "bars"`); takes the feed's figure only when the closes miss that session (bitcoin's empty Sunday bar) or hold none; refuses a quote older than a session the closes already hold ("stale quote": the board keeps the last good one, faded); and dates every price by its session (`sessionDay()`: a future's day opens at 18:00 New York time, every other market's is its exchange's calendar day, and a price on a day the market holds no session belongs to the next). Dating by the price's own day had left points no bar ever replaced: Brent "closed" on three Sundays of October, the yen on a Saturday, natural gas's 15 October rewritten with November's price. The Saudi and Egyptian indices, one bar in three months, take the feed's previous close only when the board's own record bears it out (`recordedPrev()`: the record's last close when the feed's is an earlier one, none when they cannot be matched); `mergeHistory()` drops stored points on days a market does not trade (Sunday to Thursday for those two, every day for bitcoin, Monday to Friday for the rest). The line draws the last month, as the page's note says (it drew six weeks), and the strip fades a stale quote as the board does. Self-tests pin each case on Yahoo's own answers of that night; every one fails on the old code. That change broke the currencies, found the same evening and fixed: Yahoo's daily currency bar carries roughly the price at the start of its day (over three weeks, 7 to 15 of each currency's 21 closes sat nearer the evening before than their own day's end), so the board printed the euro −0.16%, the pound −0.24% and the yen +0.32% on a Monday they had moved +0.60%, +0.51% and −0.27% from Friday's close, beside a dollar index rightly down 0.49%. A currency's closes now come from its hourly prices, each session's last (`yahooQuote()`, three months at 60 minutes), which agreed with Yahoo's own previous close for all eight; the indices', yields', futures' (their settlement) and bitcoin's daily bars close their own day and stay. The euro's self-test now holds Friday's last hourly price and fails on the daily bar.
- The currencies, 2026-11-09 (the owner: "should we expand the currencies to include more world currencies (that are not pegged against the us dollar?)", and whether «دولار/جنيه» on the strip could read as the Sudanese pound). A pound or a lira is named with its country, the strip's short label too («دولار/جنيه مصري»، «دولار/ليرة تركية»); at 720-1365px the strip leaves out the Nasdaq so the rest and the update time fit (DESIGN.md). The board adds the Swiss franc and the Indian rupee (the Gulf's largest trading partner and remittance corridor). Only floating currencies Yahoo quotes cleanly, measured over three months of daily closes: its Moroccan dirham, Tunisian dinar, Qatari riyal, Iraqi dinar and Pakistani rupee swing 2-4% every other day between two price sources (a move above 2% on 22 to 37 of 65 days), its Saudi riyal sat at 3.64 for weeks against a peg of 3.75 and printed +3.21% that day, the ruble jumps on Mondays, and a pegged currency would print 0.00% for ever. The Maghreb's currencies would need their central banks' own daily rates (Bank Al-Maghrib, the Central Bank of Tunisia); not built. The rupee's daily-move limit is 2% (one reversed pair of about 2% in August).
