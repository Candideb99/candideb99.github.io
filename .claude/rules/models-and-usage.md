# Model providers, effort levels and what the plan's allowance costs

Who writes the news, at which effort, how the model is called, and what it costs. Dated entries; where two differ, the later one stands.

- **Model providers & control**: Since 2026-10-18 the newsroom publishes with **Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`) on the owner's subscription** (provider `claude`, `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN`; his request: "switch to use claude as publisher"). The token of 2026-09-21 had been refused from 1 October with "Your organization has disabled Claude subscription access for Claude Code" (every cloud run failed until 17 October, which is why the newsroom moved to Gemini and then Codex); a new one-year token was made that day with `node scripts/claude-token.mjs`, which he runs himself: it starts `claude setup-token`, takes the pasted token, tests it with one Claude call before saving, then writes `.env` and the GitHub secret. If Claude refuses again, run it again with an account whose plan allows Claude Code. History: the owner had chosen **GPT-6.1 Sol through his ChatGPT subscription** on 2026-10-17, with no separate API billing; that route stays configured as an alternative. Provider `codex` in `pipeline/lib/llm.mjs` invokes the official Codex CLI (`lib/codex.mjs`) with high reasoning effort (`CODEX_REASONING_EFFORT`, the owner’s choice on 2026-10-17), forces ChatGPT authentication, strips API credentials, and runs without model tools. The picture desk's Claude calls think at the Extra effort since 2026-10-27 (the owner: "increase the opus 5.5 effort from high to extra in the control room, maybe it helps", on weak photos, then "do not use max"; the CLI's levels are low, medium, high, xhigh, max, and its `xhigh` is the level the Claude app's menu calls Extra; the "high" on the Settings page was Codex's). Applied to every call that morning, the first run at Extra published nothing: the writer thought past its four-minute timeout on all nine tries and the desk notes took three minutes a call (13,000 to 18,000 thinking tokens). So only the picture desk asks for it (`outside.chat` in images.mjs passes `effort`; `chat()` hands it to `callClaudeCli`, which adds `--effort` only when asked) and every other call runs at the subscription's default, as before. `pipeline/lib/claude-effort.mjs` holds the levels and the default `xhigh`; KHAZENDAR_CLAUDE_EFFORT (.env, the repository variable, passed by newsroom.yml and editor.yml) overrides it from Settings (its options named as the Claude app names them, shown when Claude is the controller; Max is not among them since 2026-10-28, the owner: "make photo selection on extra not max when using claude, as max consumes the plan very fast", so `CLAUDE_EFFORTS` leaves it out and a «max» in .env or the variable reads as Extra; the cloud logs of 27 October already showed every picture-desk call at xhigh, 6 to 9 a run at 375 to 900 thinking tokens on average against 2,000 to 5,000 for the writing's calls at the default, and nothing in the newsroom at Max: `claudeCliEnv()` strips the `CLAUDE_EFFORT=max` a desktop session at Max passes to the commands it runs); every Claude call's log line names its effort («default» or the level) and its thinking tokens. Whether the writing itself is better at Extra (the owner: "is claude put on extra better than claude on medium when writing stories/articles if we increase the timeout?") was tested blind the same day: six recent news stories with open sources, each first draft (desk notes + writer, the lessons' evidence harness `paired.mjs`) written at Medium and at Extra from the same re-fetched sources and brief, every draft judged at one effort (the blind fact-check, the critic, the house checks). Extra: 0 proved errors against 1 (a hedge dropped), 0.50 claims no source makes per story against 0.83, 28.0 supported sentences against 26.3, the critic 6.7 against 6.0 (higher in four, never lower), the same rule and style faults and length; and 16.6 minutes and 53,700 thinking tokens a draft against 3.5 minutes and 7,900. A small edge in the same direction on every measure, not proof at six stories, at nearly five times the time, which at the 35-minute run budget means about one story a run instead of three or four. Then High (the owner: "add high mode to the comparison, maybe it would be the best compromise?"), with each story written at Medium a second time to measure chance: High 2 proved errors (a cause and an attribution, one story), 1.83 claims no source makes per story, 26.2 supported sentences, the critic 7.0, 5.4 minutes and 16,200 thinking tokens a draft; Medium again 0 errors, 1.17, 24.7, the critic 5.5. Two Medium drafts of one story differed as much as the levels did (the critic 6 then 3 on the pound's story, 5 then 8 on the utilities), so at this size no level is shown better than Medium on quality, only dearer: High 1.5 times the time and twice the thinking, Extra 4.7 and 6.8 times. The writing stays at Medium. The picture desk was measured the same way on 2026-10-28 (the owner: "Run the blind photo test"): 8 stories, dry runs at Medium twice, High and Extra, the picks shown to him side by side without the levels. Extra found a photograph for 6 of the 8, Medium for 4 and 5, High for 5 (only Extra found one for Pakistan's rate rise), at 1.5 times Medium's time and 2.5 times its thinking (113 s and 7,000 thinking tokens a story against 74 s and 2,800). Where the levels differed he could not choose ("I will leave judgement to you to do what is best"), so the picture desk stays at Extra: no loss anyone could see, the most photographs found, about 40 seconds more a story. The Extra drafts used up the subscription's five-hour session allowance (refused from about noon to 12:40 UTC, "You've hit your session limit"), which the newsroom shares: no run fell in that window, and the test's harness now stops at the first such refusal. `KHAZENDAR_WRITING_EFFORT` (llm.mjs) sets the effort of every call that asks for none of its own and lengthens its timeout (High ×2, Extra ×4); unset in production. The harness and its results are in that session's scratchpad (effort-test.mjs). Local and scheduled providers are configured separately; read `.env` and GitHub Actions variables rather than assuming their current state. Gemini, Claude, the separately billed OpenAI API, Qwen and OpenRouter remain available with existing credentials preserved. Two independently authenticated ChatGPT accounts can be configured as primary/backup (`lib/codex-accounts.mjs`, `lib/codex-pool.mjs`). Only quota exhaustion uses an available backup; every exhausted five-hour/weekly window must reset before reuse. Both capped stops calls, with no paid API fallback. Subscription expiry dates in the desk are owner-entered reminders, never inferred from OAuth expiry. Cloud sessions are separate from desktop sessions and stored only in repository secrets and an authenticated encrypted cache (`scripts/codex-cloud-auth.mjs`); plaintext stays in runner temp. Routine calls retry up to three times with schema validation; pool exhaustion propagates so a news event is deferred rather than rejected. The publication thresholds and picture cascade are unchanged.
- The Claude CLI (`callClaudeCli` in `pipeline/lib/llm.mjs`) runs the CLI's native executable (`bin/claude.exe` in the npm package since 2.1.2xx; the old `cli.js` under node on older installs) with **no shell**, from a temporary directory, with `--setting-sources ""`. Never launch it through a shell: on Windows cmd.exe cuts a multi-line system prompt to its first word and expands `%…%` inside it (found 2026-09-22: every local Claude call had run with the house style missing). And never call it from the repository directory: print mode pastes this file, the owner's global CLAUDE.md and the agent memories into the writer's context.
- Where the plan's allowance goes (the owner, 2026-10-31, finding 20% of the 5-hour limit used: "probably used by the website publishing ... check and fix if there is a bug that causes tokens waste"). Measured from the plan's meter and every transcript's own token counts at API prices: 1% of the 5-hour window is about $6.5. The newsroom made no call in the window he looked at; its 33% at 16:47 UTC was Claude Code sessions (the audit session, whose context had reached ~650K tokens, re-read on each of ~400 steps: $131 in 52 minutes; its two reviewer agents $40; a film session $38). That day to 17:00 UTC: sessions about $890 (69 subagents $274 of it), the website about $45 (a news round ~$10, ~80 calls, ~$3.7 a story). The website's one real waste: the morning round searched every news story of the last two days without a photo, so each twice; the second search found one picture in 16 (23-31 October). `backfill-images.mjs` now records a search that ran to the end and found nothing (`pipeline/state/photo-searches.json`, `PHOTO_SEARCHES` in lib/health.mjs; a search the desk could not finish, `pickImage({ onError })`, is not recorded) and the round leaves those stories alone. Every Claude call's log line now carries its tokens (`in=` fresh or written to the cache, `read=`, `out=`), and the run report's `llm.byRole` totals them by role.
- Sonnet against Opus, and what the plan really costs (the owner, 2026-11-13: "should i keep using opus or switch to cheaper model like sonnet ... i wat to achieve best possible results but not overspending tokens", then "Run the test", "also test sonnet at extra", and, shown the results, "keep opus"). Consumption first, from the run reports of 9-12 November at API prices: about $4 a published news story (it was $3.7 on 31 October), $47-63 a day for all kinds, about 25 calls a published story; a news round's spend is the writer 28%, the desk notes 20%, the critic 19%, the picture desk 19%, the fact-check 8%, the editor 6%. The test: six recent long news stories (gold under $4,000, DEWA's and Telecom Egypt's nine months, the Iraqi smuggling probe, DIP Holding's debut, Orlen's cancelled LNG contract), each written from its re-fetched sources through desk notes, writer and copy desk by the arm's model, every draft judged by Opus 5.5 at the default (the blind fact-check, the critic, the house checks). Per draft: Opus at the default, twice: 2 and 2 proved errors in six stories, critic 7.2 and 7.2, $0.69 and $0.58 of writing, 186 and 152 seconds; Sonnet 5.5 at Medium: 4 errors, critic 6.5, $0.29, 84 s (and it wrote DEWA's results long where every other arm's notes sent them short); at High: 2 errors, critic 6.5, $0.35, 168 s; at Extra: no error, the fewest claims no source makes, critic 7.0, $1.57 and 857 s a draft, 2.5 times Opus's cost. Six stories cannot separate the arms (as on 27 October); the critic put Sonnet at High below both Opus runs on two stories and never above both, and sent all 30 drafts back for revision. Opus stays. If the owner ever prefers Sonnet's Arabic, the candidate is Sonnet at High for the writing steps only (notes, writer, desk: about half a round's spend), Opus kept as critic and fact-checker, which needs a model per role in `llm.mjs`; `KHAZENDAR_CLAUDE_MODEL` and `KHAZENDAR_WRITING_EFFORT` are read at each call, so a harness can switch them between calls. The plan's meter, read before and after (`get_usage`): the 5-hour window went from 8% to 72% and the week from 20% to 28% while about $60-85 was spent (the test $34.66, two Claude Code sessions about $12 each, one analysis run), so 1% of the 5-hour window is now about $1 and 1% of the week $8-10, not the $6.5 of 31 October (the plan reads «Team»); the newsroom is therefore a third to a half of the weekly allowance. I had quoted the test as "about 5% of one window" from the old figure; it took about a third. Read the meter again before quoting any test's cost as a share of the plan.
