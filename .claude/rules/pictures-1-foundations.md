# The picture desk: how it works (September to 22 October 2026)

The cascade, the rules every judge reads, the country rule, the libraries. Dated entries in the order they were learned; where two differ, the later one stands (the skyline ban of 28 September, for one, was narrowed on 7 November). Continued in pictures-2 and pictures-3.

- A story's photograph must come from the story's own country whenever its Commons file says where it was taken (the owner, 2026-09-23, on a Finnish filling station illustrating a US diesel story). `pipeline/lib/images.mjs` enforces it twice: `PLACE_RULE` in every model prompt that chooses or checks a picture, and `placedAbroad()` in code, which reads the file's title, description and categories against the countries the story's headline and tags name (their adjectives and waters too, «مضيق هرمز», and the seats of the world bodies and companies in it; its desks count only when it names none, because a desk is a broad shelf: the Asia desk let a China–Russia story keep an Australian jetty) and refuses a photo placed elsewhere. A story on the world desk alone is left to the models and may keep an honest place in its caption. When nothing from the story's country passes, a neutral frame runs as «صورة تعبيرية» and its caption names no place (the illustration's own caption writer writes that caption before the second check reads it, and the pass has two tries: the first في العمق piece lost a fitting tanker to a caption naming its Norwegian port); `asIllustrationIfElsewhere()` applies that to every pick and `node pipeline/recaption-images.mjs` to published ones. `node pipeline/audit-images.mjs` re-checks every published photo; `node pipeline/backfill-images.mjs --redo=slug,…` re-picks. A story about named people is illustrated with those people first (the owner, 2026-09-23, on the White House facade under a Trump–Xi summit story: "boring"): the writer's first photo search names them, the fallback asks who they are (`people`) and searches them together, a file naming two of them ranks first, an earlier occasion is accepted by both checks (never a wrong person), the geography rule does not apply to them, and `markFilePhoto()` makes an older photo of officials say so in its caption (the occasion's year, or «(أرشيفية)»). Newest first (the owner, same day: "check if we can still get up to date images"): `searchCommons()` keeps the search's own order (`index`; page-id order had put 2017 files ahead of May 2026's), `photoYear()` reads when a photo was taken (record, else file name; never the upload year for the pre-2005 refusal), `recencyScore()` falls off year by year, and the people search asks for this year first. Fresher and livelier (Task 3, 2026-09-24: of the newest 24 stories, 12 ran photos taken 2007-2018, most of them building fronts, skylines, a satellite view or a portrait): the writer's own subjects and the people search also ask for this year's files (`collect()` `fresh`; not generic sector scenes, where "oil pipeline 2026" fetched unrelated files), a story naming two people also searches each of them alone, a mild `liveliness()` weight moves people at work and sectors in action ahead of facades, skylines, satellite views and studio portraits, the judges are told to prefer life (`LIVELY_RULE`), an institution's story may be illustrated by its head at work, captioned as a file photo (`genericQueries()` people), and a person's photo more than eight years old is dropped (`peoplePhoto()`). Relevance leads (2026-09-24, the owner on an offshore platform's risers under a Brent story, "weird", and on the Treasury's front under an explainer of bond yields): the ranking had read recency and life but not relevance, so a 2026 beach beat a fitting tanker; now a file must name at least half of what its search phrase is about (`relevance()`, lower-case words only: capitalised ones are places and names), an event's photograph (forum, conference, ceremony) never serves as a generic scene, every judge refuses a frame that does not read at a glance (`GLANCE_RULE`, `glanceFaults()`, shared with the audit), a piece that explains or analyses is pictured by its subject at work and never by a building front (`CONCEPT_RULE`, the hub writers' `HUB_IMAGE_QUERIES`), and a shortlist the judge turns down whole is passed over for the next four (`twoLooks()`). Captions keep the house spellings (`fixNames()`).
- Photographs are hotlinked from Wikimedia Commons, which serves only the widths 120/250/330/500/960/1280; `src/lib/wikimedia.ts` builds the srcset and any other width answers HTTP 400. Since 2026-09-24 (the owner: "add a second free photo library or even third or fourth") the picture desk also searches `pipeline/lib/photolibs.mjs`: Openverse with no key (Flickr's openly licensed photos and a few collections; 20 searches a minute and 200 a day anonymously, so it is asked in the generic passes for a pass's first two searches and whenever Commons has fewer than five fitting pictures, and left alone for the rest of a run after a 429), and Pexels and Unsplash when the owner sets their free keys (`PEXELS_API_KEY`, `UNSPLASH_ACCESS_KEY`, in .env and as repository secrets; Unsplash's use is reported as its terms ask). NC and ND licences are never asked for. Two of every six photos the judge sees come from these libraries when they offered any (their photos carry no date, and the ranking alone kept them behind Commons' dated but unusable files); the credit names the library (فليكر، أوبن فيرس، بيكسلز، أنسبلاش) and the site sizes Flickr, Pexels and Unsplash copies too.
- Photographs are serious (the owner, 2026-09-27, on a horse-drawn fuel cart at a Cairo pump under an analysis of Egypt's central bank: "some pictures are laughable and do not make the website look serious"): `GRAVITY_RULE` in `pipeline/lib/images.mjs` is read by every choosing pass, the second check (verdict UNSERIOUS) and both audits, and `noveltyFault()` refuses in code a file or caption that names an animal, a cart, a toy, a costume, a cartoon or a mural the story is not about. LIVELY_RULE's life is the economy at work, never a curiosity. `node pipeline/audit-gravity.mjs` looks at every published photo beside its headline (six to a call) and names those to re-pick with `backfill-images.mjs --redo=`.
- What the picture desk learned from measuring itself (the owner, 2026-09-27, beside Al-Borsa's photo of a man counting Egyptian pounds and ours of the Nile under the same inflation story: "are you sure that the logic for images is superior than before?"; he proposed reading the sources' own photos). Three blind rounds on the same 20 stories, every photo scored 1-5 by Claude vision beside its headline, arms shuffled: the live picker 2.85-2.95, the fixed picker 3.00-3.05, the fixed picker with the sources' photos as the brief 3.00-3.05 (first pass or pooled), the photos already published 3.05-3.20; the judge's own scores move about 0.15 between runs, so no picker was measurably better and none beat what is published. What the reasons showed, and is now live: the leader's file photo is chosen first only when a person is the news (`focus` from `genericQueries()`; Tim Cook at an EU meeting over a US patent verdict and Bessent at a podium over sanctions on Iranian aviation scored 2 where an Iran Air airliner scored 5), the writers are told the same for `image_queries`; people paying or counting money, or the country's banknotes, may picture prices, inflation, a currency or wages (the rules had forbidden the owner's own example); warships are dropped in code on civilian stories (`WARSHIP`, the USS Porter's Hormuz collision crowded out tanker searches); refusals for what a frame shows follow it into the last pass (`HARD_REFUSALS`); an oil field's «nodding donkey» is not an animal; the inliner reads only JPEG, PNG, WebP and GIF. The sources'-photos brief did not earn its extra call as the FIRST brief (free copies of an agency's subject are mostly amateur); the trial harness, data and blind scores stay on the local branch `wip/source-photo-brief` in `experiments/photo-trial/`, and since 2026-09-28 the brief runs as the cascade's second tier (next bullet). The ceiling is the photo supply, not the picker: Pexels and Unsplash (the owner's free keys) or a paid agency feed would raise it; rerun that trial after either. Trade stories (the owner, 2026-09-27, beside Al-Youm Al-Sabea's oranges on a packing line and Mubasher's crates of citrus, where Egypt's food-exports story ran a foreign cheese dairy's curd vat as «صورة تعبيرية»): a sector's exports or imports are pictured by its goods on their way, the country's best-known products packed, loaded or on sale, never a factory of one product the story does not name; among the searches always those goods in the country's own markets or fields («fruit market Cairo»), because Commons holds far more of a country's markets and farms than of its packing houses; and the second check takes the country's own goods of a broadly named sector as GENERIC_OK (it had refused a Cairo fruit market as "fresh produce, not processed food"). Rules in `genericQueries()`, `lastResort()` and `verifyImage()` in images.mjs and the writer's `image_queries` in write.mjs; the story was re-picked (a fruit kiosk in Cairo). Flickr's servers refuse some thumbnails to a bot that names itself (403), so the judge never saw them; the shortlist then asks Openverse's own thumbnail of the same photo (`thumbAlt`), never a disguised user agent. The neutral pass refuses writing in a script that is not the story's own, even small (Chinese characters on crates). Credits: Commons' Artist field is cleaned in code (`cleanArtist()` in commons.mjs: the name it assumes in «No machine-readable author provided. X assumed», the creator a sentence names, a Flickr uploader without the city, a long credit ended at a comma, never mid-word); `recaption-images.mjs --spellings` applies it to published credits (ten fixed that night). Since 2026-10-28 (found in the blind photo test of the picture desk's effort: «You may select the license of your choice.» stood in a file's Artist field, its credit, Tasnim News Agency, in the Attribution field the desk never asked for), a licence sentence or a placeholder («[null Courtesy]») gives no name, and labels, talk links, «at Arabic Wikipedia», a derivative's second author, Flickr's city with its state, emoji and doubled marks are cut. `creditName()` reads the Artist field, then Attribution, then the body a release names (a Pentagon photo's «United States Department of Defense»). Attribution comes second because, read first, it changed eight good credits of 276 («… or alternatively © …», «© European Union, 2026» for a named photographer). Pexels, Unsplash and Openverse names are cleaned too. `--spellings` now re-reads each published Commons photo's record: 20 credits fixed, no other line changed.
- The picture desk is a cascade, each tier tried in full before the next (the owner, 2026-09-28, on a foreign concrete frame as «صورة تعبيرية» under Egypt's draft law binding real-estate developers' money to their projects: "this means a picture about new administrative capital building being built or new building project in egypt … same location of the news is priority, so you get cascading priorities"; and the same hour: "make claude read the news too to understand context … not just from the news title"). `findImage()` in images.mjs: (0) the story's people when a named person is the news; (1) the story's own place at its most specific (`places` and `place_queries` from `genericQueries()`: a named site, or the places a country's sector is best known for, the New Administrative Capital, New Cairo and New Alamein for Egypt's developers, Ras Laffan for Qatar's LNG; only files whose own record names one, `namesAPlace()`, which reads «Tenth» as «10th» and never lets «New Cairo» match «Cairo, IL»; the judge and the second check are told that the place showing the story's subject is the first choice and not a generic skyline, `placeTierNote()`); (2) the subject of the sources' own lead photographs (`sourceBrief()`: Claude vision reads up to three og:images, never published, and writes library searches for what the best one shows; a logo, a graphic, a meeting or a portrait is no brief); (3) the writer's own `image_queries`; (4) the subject in its country, then the story's people when the news is a thing; (5) the last resort and the neutral «صورة تعبيرية». Every step that plans, chooses or checks a picture reads the whole story (`storyText()`: lede, body, «لماذا يهمّ», box), where it had read the headline, the dek and 400 characters of the lede, and the planner also reads the first 1,200 characters of up to three sources' own reports. A story never runs a frame of a numbered series another story runs (`seriesKey()`: «Central business district 1, New Administrative Capital», which the Savills story runs, and «Central business district, …» are one picture; a year or a date is not a frame number, so two plain «New York Stock Exchange» files by different photographers stay two pictures). The newsroom passes its sources to `pickImage()`; `backfill-images.mjs` reads the sources' pages again (`--no-sources` skips them) and its dry run prints the caption and credit. Tested the same night on 11 stories (dry runs, then applied with the owner's "Publish all"): the place tier found a tanker photographed in the Strait of Hormuz for the tanker-rates story (it had run without a photo since the gravity audit) and the Housing Ministry in the New Capital for the developers' draft law (the business district's towers already run on the Savills story of the same day); the sources'-photos tier found trucks in a port yard for SCZone's Sokhna truck yard and a tanker at an oil terminal for the East-West pipeline story; the rest fell to the subject tiers as before (Pexels refineries, a price board, a trading floor as «صورة تعبيرية»). Part of the gain on stories that had no photo is Pexels, which the cloud newsroom could search only from 2026-09-27 22:12 UTC. Re-picking stories that already had a photo gave nothing better (Yanbu, Hull, OpenAI), so none was replaced. Cost: the planner's call on every story (it used to run only when the writer's searches failed), one vision call when the sources carry photographs, and the place tier's looks when the story has a place.
- The picture desk's stress test (the owner, 2026-09-28: "further improve image engine ... it might still be buggy, i think you have to further stress test it more"). Two halves. (1) **Code, no model**: `scripts/images-selftest.mjs`, in the gate, runs the whole cascade on scripted stand-ins for Claude, Commons, the libraries and the thumbnails (`withStandIns()` in images.mjs; every outside call goes through its `outside` table): the place tier first and only files that name the place, the repeat guard, «New Cairo» against «Cairo, IL», the sources'-photo tier and a logo never downloaded, the second check failing closed, vision down (the metadata judge), every pass refusing (the neutral frame), the planner down, accented names, location before likeness, a skyline for the last resort alone, and every library down at once. It found, before any model call: accented names dropped by ASCII-only patterns (Erdoğan read as «Erdo», Şimşek losing his surname, the AKP deputy Sayan Kaya never searched: `fold()`, `PERSON_NAME`, a Unicode `RANK`); places opening with a number dropped («10th of Ramadan City»); a plan with people but no scene searches thrown away whole; «Cairo, Illinois» passing as Egypt because it names Cairo (`NAMESAKE`: a name followed by a US state, its postal code or a Canadian province places the file there; Georgia only by its code); and `pickImage()` able to throw away a written, checked story on any exception (it now returns no photo instead). (2) **Live, dry runs on 32 published stories judged as if new** (`backfill-images.mjs --dry-run --as-new`: the story's own photo stays a candidate, no refusals carried between stories; every pick looked at beside the photo it would replace). Found and fixed: the planner named where news was decided as the story's place (Apple's patent verdict ran its San Diego courthouse: a place is where the subject is, never a court, ministry, parliament, headquarters or venue, `NOT_A_PLACE`, and a company's result, product, valuation, lawsuit or deal has none); a skyline taken before the market searches ran (the rule "a skyline only when no market, money or bank is among the candidates" was judged within one pass, which a cascade breaks: `SKYLINE_LAST`, only `lastResort()` passes `skyline: true`; Egypt's rate decision had run the 6th of October bridge and the Cairo Tower); the sources'-photo tier taking any subject and any country (it is told the subject it may take, `briefTierNote()`, and for a story about one country takes only files placed there, so Oman's growth story got Duqm's dry dock instead of an unplaced Pexels tanker); camera numbers read as years («IMG_1945», «IMG_1871», photographs of the Egyptian Exchange, dropped as archival); a person whose every photograph is older than eight years left without one (Warren Buffett stepping down: the older ones, up to fifteen years, are tried after the recent ones fail, captioned with their year); and Pexels' one-sentence descriptions held to half a search's words (the sovereign-rating explainer lost sixteen trader photos before any judge saw them; a third for Pexels and Unsplash); and the right frame refused for its caption (four A321neo photographs refused in one run for naming AirAsia, Cathay, United and Air Astana, and the Airbus story fell to its headquarters front: the second check may now answer RECAPTION, the caption is written again to name only what the story names, `writeCaption({ plainNames })`, and read once more; tested live, the Turkish Airlines A321neo then passed as «طائرة إيرباص «إيه321 نيو» على مدرج أحد المطارات»). After the fixes, all 32 again: 27 with a photo, against the photographs they run now 7 better (Buffett's 2015 file photo, the sovereign-rating explainer, F-35s at Prince Sultan Air Base, AlUla, Tehran's bazaar, two skylines gone), 19 the same or level (Sharara, Ras Laffan, Salalah, the Trump–Xi file photo, a Cairo market for the rate decision), 6 worse (two left empty that found a photo in the round before, the Bank of Japan's own building for a Tokyo price board, AMD's chief executive for a server rack, Google's Hamina data centre seen from across the water, Airbus before the RECAPTION fix): the judges vary between runs, so a single run's pick is not a verdict; the photographs the site runs had the writer's own searches, which a dry run of a published story cannot replay. Median 41 seconds and 5 Claude calls a story (at most 135 s and 14), about 3 Openverse and 3 Pexels searches a story, inside Openverse's 200 a day at 20 stories. Examples are not rules (the owner, the same night, on a Cairo market under the rate decision: "relevant but i am not sure if serious enough to be related to this headline, unless the article mentioned multiple times the effects on egyptian livelihood ... this was just an example, not a rule"): a central bank's decision on rates is pictured by the bank itself, its governor or its money photographed as money (notes counted, stacked, in hand), and an everyday market belongs to stories about prices and what households pay; the planner is told its examples are examples; a note's or a coin's face is refused in code (`SCAN`: «… obverse», «Backside»; `DEPICTION`: Commons categories «… in art», «Banknotes with a face value of …», which «One Egyptian pound note», both faces scanned, carried); for a monetary story the last resort names the country's money before any skyline. Commons holds one photograph of the Central Bank of Egypt's building, 893 pixels wide, under the desk's 1000-pixel floor. A picture editor's taste, the same night (the owner, on the governor "delivering a speech somewhere" and an amateur snapshot of pound notes: "usually sophisticated newspapers choose images that also deliver a message in itself"): the planner states what the picture should say (`message`, the story's point, not its topic, and `message_queries`, which lead the subject tier), and every judge of every pass is told it (`messageNote()`: choose the photograph that says it; one that only names the topic is the weaker choice, one that says something else is not chosen), a publication standard (`PUBLICATION_STANDARD`: a professional's frame, never an amateur snapshot, a scan or a photographed screen), and that people run in the role the story is about (a governor at the bank or its rate announcement; one speaking at an unrelated event only when the story is about them personally). Grounded in The Economist's picture desk ("understand what it is really saying, and find imagery that informs, engages and sometimes surprises", "to publication standard", its advertisement of 16 September 2026) and the Bonn Institute's selection guidelines ("make sure the meaning is clear, not vague", "refrain from using a picture as a mere illustration of the issue"). No pass takes a skyline any more, the last resort included (`SKYLINE_LAST`; it had still run a Nile corniche under the rate decision once the governor and the snapshot were refused). That was my rule, stricter than his of 24 September ("a skyline last"); since 2026-11-07 the city pass alone takes a city's business district, under a story about that city's own stock market (see "The city pass"). The honest cost: the free libraries hold little that says an Egyptian or Gulf monetary story (the rate hold and the US-yields analysis found nothing that met the bar: Cairo, Georgia; British pounds; Samoa's central bank), so such a story runs without a photograph rather than with a weak one. Pixabay was tried with the owner's key (kept in `.env` only) and not added: its API forbids permanent hotlinking ("download them to your server first", links expire in 24 hours), asks for no automated queries, overlaps Pexels on generic subjects and has nothing of the places the desk now searches (the New Administrative Capital returned Hanover's town hall; «Cairo market», tourist bazaars).
- The picture desk, 2026-10-18 (the owner, beside الشرق بلومبرغ's gloved hand of phosphate granules and CNBC عربية's ministry photo of the meeting, under our hazy phosphate-mine gate for the Fertiglobe story: "if we really did fix the image choosing"). - The picture desk, 2026-10-22 (the owner, on the front page's cover: "picture choices is still bit poor i guess,, we need to improve the logic behind image choices").
