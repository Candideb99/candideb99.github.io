# Sources and selection, continued again

Continues sources-and-coverage-2.md, which neared its size limit on 2026-11-23. Dated entries; where two differ, the later one stands.

- Codex's second review of 2026-11-23, what reaches the editor and three of the site's rules (the owner: "also check what codex says here", ten findings). All ten real: three reproduced with probes on the live code (the repeat check, the price-table filter, the reversal), the rest read in the code. No model call. Pinned in `scripts/weights-selftest.mjs` (the lib functions on their cases; run.mjs's own steps and the site's rules by their source lines). Worked in a worktree at the live code. (1) **A follow-up was stopped on its headline**: `repeatsRecent()` took «رئيس الوزراء العراقي يقيل 9 مسؤولين إثر تحقيق في تهريب النفط بجرف الصخر» for a repeat of the order to investigate, two days before, and the story was skipped before the desk notes, the desk built on 15 November to read what happened since. A headline that reads as a published one now goes to the notes with that story as the one it follows (`follows: repeated`); they answer what is new, or null and the story is not written. Only a refresh and a flagship report, which get no such notes, are still stopped on the headline. Cost: a true repeat now pays for one notes call before it is dropped; the bet is that real follow-ups are worth more than that. Not measured: how often the notes call a true repeat a development. (2) **One headline from two outlets was one item**: the intake kept the first feed to answer, so a central bank's release and a paper's report of it under one headline left the event with one source or the other by chance. The key is the outlet and the headline. (3) **A forecast closed a release**: `coveredBy()` (reports.mjs) took any headline carrying the release's name within ten days. It must be a news story, look ahead to nothing (`AHEAD`), and have run no earlier than 36 hours before the desk first saw the edition (`since`, from state/reports.json). The saved record crediting an explainer with October's Saudi prices was left as it is: a news story had told it. (4) **The price-table filter took news**: a headline with a percentage, a decision or a deal is news whatever else it says (`serviceItem()`, now pipeline/lib/intake.mjs). Its two cases were Codex's test headlines; the run reports do not keep what the filter drops, so how much real news it took is unknown. (5) **A feed's places went to its oldest items**: `fetchFeed()` cut the feed's own order to `maxItems`, and items already used held places. `feedShare()` gives the places to the newest first, never to one the newsroom has used or decided on (`skip`). (6) **A reversal was a repeat**: gold up 4%, then down 6% an hour later, was held by the eight-hour rule, which knew a reversal only by the word. `repeatsInstrument()` (context.mjs) reads each headline's direction in its own clause (the cause clause set aside) and lets an opposite move through. A small move the other way passes too; the editor's bar for a market move is what holds that. (7) The stories a run has published count for that rule (`RUN_PUBLISHED`). (8) **Trade and reserve figures were quotes**: `isMarketQuote()` (src/lib/markets.ts) kept India's gold imports and the Chinese central bank's gold purchases out of the front's figures; imports, exports, purchases, sales, holdings, output, stocks and demand are no quote. (9) «اقرأ أيضاً» reads a tag as its file (`canonicalTag()`), so two spellings of one file tie their stories. (10) A section's block on the front looks for its photographed lead among all its fresh stories before it is cut to four; no occurrence in the archive. Not seen in a run: any of it.
