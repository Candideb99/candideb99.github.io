# Sources, feeds, the reports desk and what the editor takes

Which outlets the newsroom reads and why, how pages and reports are read, and the editor's scope. Dated entries; where two differ, the later one stands.

News evidence (2026-10-17): `pipeline/lib/news-evidence.mjs` preserves official-source metadata and
may supplement the selected reports with one relevant institutional document explicitly linked in
their readable bodies. Exact approved hosts, document paths, robots and redirect checks apply;
failure keeps the original evidence, and the five-source limit remains. Publication metadata is
not an event date. The shared editorial brief requires independent construction, honest reporting
origins and sourced expert identification, never invented reporting or novelty. See the dated audit.
- The reports desk (the owner, 2026-10-18: "should the website follow reports like [the UBS Global Wealth Report]?", then "yes build it, however although arab world is in focus (not only gulf states), but important developed economies are also important like germany, france etc"): `--mode=reports` (daily 08:17 UTC; `--report=<id>` for one) reads new editions of the flagship reports in `pipeline/reports.json` from their publishers' own pages, as primary sources (`kind: "report"`, counted like an official source), and writes them through the ordinary path (notes, writer, desk, critic, mend, precheck, picture desk) via `produceStory({ preset })`. `pipeline/lib/reports.mjs`: an edition is detected from LINKS (text, title or address; a future-dated one ignored) or from a page's own text, or from an address pattern (`template`, the IEA's); the edition's page, its full report (a "Full Report" link, a PDF read with `unpdf` 1.8.1 or the IMF's Markdown version; never an annex, a country note, a methodology note or another language: `bestPdf()`) and its press release (`pressRelease()`, for the headline numbers) become up to three sources: the summary, the passages on the economies (`reportExcerpts()`: the Arab economies weighted three to one over the major ones, each tagged [p.N]) and the release. The brief (`reportStory()`) leads with the finding that matters most to Arab readers, all the Arab economies the report covers and then the major economies. State `pipeline/state/reports.json`: a report's first sight records its edition as the starting point unless it is this month's; a failed edition is tried once more the next day; one already told (same address, or the report's name in a recent title) is skipped; at most two a run. 17 reports live, 13 listed as `disabled` with the reason (bot walls: OPEC, OECD, UNCTAD, the Energy Institute, the OBR, ESCWA, the AfDB, the CBO, ifo; a 403: the Arab Monetary Fund; no stable page: the EBRD; the WTO's outlook arrives through its own feed). Tested live: the World Bank's October 2026 MENAAP update became a 424-word story led by Gulf oil output, covering Egypt, Jordan, Morocco, Tunisia, Lebanon, Yemen and Iraq, fact-check clean.
- Politics that moves money (the owner, 2026-10-22, after asking why the Houthi war on Saudi Arabia and Libya's ratification of its maritime deal with Turkey had no story, then whether to widen into politics for AdSense: "Stay economics-first, but cover politics and conflict when they move money, from the economic angle"). Three causes, all fixed. (1) Scope: the general feeds were cut to their business pages. Arab News (/middle-east/, /saudi-arabia/), The National (/news/mena|gulf|uae/) and Al Jazeera Arabic (/news/) now join, Sky News Arabia's Middle East desk is added (`skynewsarabia-me`, the same outlet as its business feed, so never two outlets), and `CANDIDATE_CAP` is 450 (about 620 fresh items; 300 gave each feed its nine freshest). The editor's rule «POLITICS AND CONFLICT WHEN THEY MOVE MONEY» (`select.mjs`) and the writer's (`write.mjs`) take an attack, sanction, treaty or ratification only when the items tie it to output, facilities, shipping, flights, drilling rights, trade, investment, a budget or a price, told and filed by that consequence (energy's guide in sections.json names drilling rights and the maritime boundaries that decide them); battlefield news, diplomacy and condemnations are not taken, and a treaty that grants rights is news without figures. (2) Broken feeds: BBC Arabic's economy filter had matched nothing (its addresses carry no section) and is gone; Yahoo Finance (404) and Al Khaleej (403 to the named reader) are disabled; Independent Arabia's feed has not moved since 20 October. (3) Chosen stories thrown away by mechanics: the last cut (`cutToSources()` in verify.mjs, `lastCut()` in run.mjs) removes a revised draft's sentences carrying a figure no source has, and a stitched second event to the end of its paragraph, instead of rejecting the story (a figure in the headline, dek or a subhead, or a stitch in the lede, still rejects; the critic's second pass and the check before publication read the cut draft); `sameEvent()` (lib/events.mjs) no longer joins two subjects through their cause clause alone (Brent's five-month high was skipped as a repeat of the investment conference's postponement) or two actors with different figures (the IMF's Gulf outlook, skipped twice as the World Bank's); the four true repeats among 290 published stories still match. Tested: self-tests; a live editor call on that evening's 450 candidates took the Aramco and Dammam attacks as energy, the IMF outlook and Libya's central bank, and passed over the condemnations; a focused call took the Turkey–Libya deal as energy (drilling rights) at 7.

Independent Arabia did not block us: Cloudflare served its feed 65 hours stale to every reader, browsers included, and its robots.txt disallows /rss/ anyway. It is now read from its Google News sitemap (the one its robots.txt names). `feeds.mjs` reads news sitemaps (`urlset` with `news:news`), filters Arabic addresses decoded, and `hourKey` asks a publisher whose cache has stopped refreshing once an hour under a new key (the reader still names itself). Added after testing about forty candidates with the newsroom's own reader (fresh items, robots, readable bodies): الاقتصادية (`aleqt`, news sitemap, sport and culture left out), the Anadolu Agency's Arabic economy feed, Gulf News (business, Gulf and MENA, news sitemap), البيان (economy, news sitemap), and Bloomberg (economics, markets) and the FT (markets, Middle East) as paywalled headline-and-summary second outlets. Still closed: Al Arabiya, Argaam, Mubasher, Al Ain, Ahram, Egypt Today, S&P Global (403 or a bot wall); the WSJ's public feeds were empty; Asharq Business, CNBC Arabia and Zawya name no news sitemap. Not added: DW, France 24 and Euronews Arabic, Okaz and Al Ittihad (general news with little economics). Five old feeds sit under a robots Disallow (EIA, Gulf Times, Eurostat, the World Bank papers, arXiv's API); they are the publishers' own subscription feeds and were left, since the robots rule here governs article pages. A live editor call drew from the new sources in all six of its stories.
- The world's press (the owner, 2026-10-25: should Khazendar add «قراءات في صحف العالم», since its news "maybe covering only western perspective"; then "test candidate economic outlets from Turkey, Iran, India, China, Japan and Europe"; then "add the readable Turkish, Iranian and Indian outlets with ownership labels, the subscribers-only check and the Persian quote fix"). Measured first: of 982 sources behind 317 news stories, 56% were Arab outlets, 40% Western, 2 Turkish and none Iranian, Indian or Chinese; none of 18 Iran, 18 China or 9 Turkey stories cited that country's own press; 80 stories credited Reuters, Bloomberg, AFP or AP through an Arab outlet (Reuters is no source of ours); India led one headline in 364 articles. So the gap was sourcing, not opinion: a press-review section was advised against (a compiled digest like «في العمق», the source-after-source habit, opinion with no reporter), and a weekly one-question piece left for later. 76 outlets were probed with the newsroom's own reader: Turkey, Iran and India open; China and Japan closed in practice (state English only; Caixin, Nikkei, NHK, Kyodo walled or feedless); Europe's business papers paywalled article by article and mostly opting out of Claude-User. Added (`sources.json`, each note dated): Bloomberg HT, Habertürk, Dünya, Ekonomim, Hürriyet Daily News, Daily Sabah, Anadolu's English desk; Tejarat News, Shana, Tehran Times, Mehr, Iran International; The Economic Times, BusinessLine, The Financial Express, Moneycontrol, each read on its economy desk (Dünya and FE by path, Tejarat News by its feed's categories with its price tables left out, FE and Moneycontrol from their news sitemaps, Tehran Times tp/697, Mehr tp/557, Iran International's economy, sanctions and energy items); `CANDIDATE_CAP` 600 (at 450 the new feeds took 132 places from the current ones). **Labels** (`lib/outlets.mjs`): `owner`, `firstMention` and `mustSay` on a source's entry, `outletLabels` for agencies the sources quote (IRNA, Fars, Tasnim, Press TV, TRT); the writer sees an OUTLET line under each labelled source and the labels of the quoted outlets its sources name; HOUSE_STYLE has WHOSE VOICE (STYLE.md §1 too), the copy desk keeps a description whole, and `programmaticChecks` sends back a story whose first mention of a state, ministry, pro-government or opposition outlet (its name after an outlet noun or a reporting verb, so «شرق الأناضول» and «شهر مهر» are no outlets) carries none of its `mustSay` words; a private paper's country is not checked. **Subscribers-only** (`subscribersOnly()` in extract.mjs): a page marked `isAccessibleForFree: false` gives no text, as a paywalled feed's would; among the current sources it moved AGBI and The Verge to headline and summary (4 of the 4 newest cited pages marked; Bloomberg's carry it too, already headline-only), and NYT's pages now answer our reader 403. **Persian quotes**: `tokens()` in factcheck.mjs folds ی ک ۀ into ي ك ه (a Persian quote in Arabic letters was not found before). **Numbers**: `sourceNumbers()` in util.mjs reads a source's figures as its language writes them (a decimal comma, dot thousands, «4 bin 500», a Persian decimal slash, Turkish and Persian scale words) for `ungroundedNumbers`, the fact-check's `missingFigures` (drift) and the saved evidence passages: the first Turkish dry run (Audi's $4.2bn US plant, three Turkish outlets) failed its notes and its writer three times each because «4,2» counted as 42; after the fix the same story went through (26 desk notes, critic 7 then 8, a clean check) with its labels («بحسب صحيفة «ديلي صباح» التركية المقربة من الحكومة»), though its critic had called them descriptions no source gives: the critic now sees each OUTLET line and is told the label is required. CHECKER_VERSION hashes `quoteFound`, `verifyStory` and the prompts, not these helpers, and stayed 7bb3653e29: the lessons' pairs did not restart, and no pair's error count can change (drift and confirmed both count, weak and supported neither). `--source=` takes several feeds, since a story needs two outlets. Tested: self-tests (the marker, Persian quotes, numbers, labels); an Iranian story through the newsroom's own steps (Shana's Persian text and Tehran Times, on Iran at the gas exporters' forum before the Moscow summit) carried both labels from its first draft and checked clean (29 checks, 7 quoting the Persian text, 6 of them found); a dry run on the five Iranian feeds alone chose nothing (domestic items, no event two of them shared). Not done: Donya-e-Eqtesad (its all-news feed is mostly other sites' items, its own feeds were not found), Eghtesad Online (the extractor keeps part of its text), Global Times (no live feed), and the opt-out question for current sources: 12 of the 32 most cited block Anthropic's agents, CNBC, the NYT and the Guardian Claude-User too, and the reader obeys only `*` and its own name. His ruling the same day: "you do not have to respect any publisher who blocks Anthropic's agents if we do not breach any license, if mentioning source is enough to avoid any legal consequence then it is fine i guess". That is what the reader already does, so nothing changed; paywalls, robots rules for every reader and the subscribers-only marker still hold, and the writer still reports facts in its own words. Two cautions given with it: crediting a source does not make copied wording lawful (the copy checks and the desk notes are what keep it out), and in the EU a machine-readable opt-out can reserve a publisher's text-and-data-mining rights (the copyright directive's Article 4), so an EU publisher that blocks Anthropic's agents is not added without a look.

- World coverage, measured 2026-11-09 (the owner: "does the website get economics and business and trade news from europe, asia, south america etc or very poor in this regard and should expand its sources?"). Of 273 news stories from 12 October to 9 November, 99 had no Arab desk: about 50 on the US (17 on its tech companies, Anthropic alone 4), 19 on Europe (the ECB, the BoE, the euro area's data, France's debt, Shell, BP, Barclays; Germany's own economy once, its gas storage), about 20 on Asia (China about 10, the Bank of Japan, Korea's exports, Samsung, SK Hynix, Indonesia, Pakistan, India only through Jio's listing), 3 on Latin America (Brazil's election twice, Venezuela), none on sub-Saharan Africa. One 36-hour read of every feed (892 items) held about 135 items on the US, 70 China, 36 Britain, 33 the euro area, 14 France, 12 India, 11 Japan, 9 Latin America (mostly Bloomberg one-liners, which cannot be written from), 8 Germany, 6 Africa. The editor's proximity rule (`select.mjs`) names the Arab world and "the global forces that move them: oil, the dollar, the Fed, the ECB, China, trade, technology", so Germany, Japan, India or Brazil compete on size alone. Germany is the rule's doing, not supply's: Destatis (in German) and the Bundesbank are already sources, and that day's pool held German exports and the trade surplus, which the editor passed over. Built the same evening at his word ("Yes, do both"). The editor's brief (`select.mjs`) counts the other large economies under القرب and has THE WORLD'S LARGE ECONOMIES, NOT ONLY AMERICA AND CHINA: a large economy's central-bank decision or headline figure ranks ahead of a second story within days on the same company, and is taken when the recent stories hold none. One call each on the same pool of 600 (n=1): the old rules took a third story in three days on Nvidia, the new took Germany's September exports from Destatis and Hürriyet Daily News, the other three picks the same. Six sources joined, each noted in `sources.json`: the Bank of Japan, the Reserve Bank of India and Brazil's central bank (official, so one of them carries a story alone), MercoPress for the Southern Cone, and BusinessDay and Nairametrics for Nigeria (two, since one news outlet never carries a story alone; BusinessDay's main feed held its five newest items, opinion and sport, so its economy desk's is read). Three per-source options came with them: `excludeTitle` (`feeds.mjs`; the Reserve Bank's latest ten releases are nearly all auctions and money-market notices, and its addresses say nothing), `readPdf` (the Bank of Japan's statements, outlook and minutes are PDFs only, read with the reports desk's `pdfPages()`: 4,051 to 9,000 characters; its speeches' own pages hold 180 and are left out) and `feedText` (Brazil's central bank draws its pages with a script the reader cannot run, and its feed carries each note whole), the last two in `collectNewsEvidence()`, all three pinned by self-tests that fail on the old code. The check before publication reads the text gathered then; the second look, which cannot fetch those pages again, reads the copy saved at publication. A Destatis English feed was not found (404). The Turkish, Iranian and Indian feeds of 25 October, due for judgment about 8 November: 11 of 16 cited at least once; never The Economic Times, BusinessLine, The Financial Express (403 to GitHub's runners), Tehran Times, Iran International. Kept: a run's pool (564 items on 9 November) is under its cap of 600, so they crowd nothing out. Measured by `node scripts/coverage.mjs --ref=pages/main` (after `git fetch pages`; it leaves the working tree alone): the news stories of the 28 days before the push of 9 November 18:25 UTC against those since, by the places their headlines name (the pipeline's `countriesNamed()`, which `regions.mjs` now shares with `placeRe()` and `countryText()`, plus what its list lacks: South Africa, the euro area, the Bank of England as «بنك إنجلترا», the Fed as «الفيدرالي», markets and currencies; North Korea is not the rule's Korea, «بيروت» not Peru), Fisher's exact test on the shares, and the six sources' citations and feeds.
