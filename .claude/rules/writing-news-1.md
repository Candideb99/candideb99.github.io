# Writing news: depth, headlines, the spine (September to 1 November 2026)

How a news story is written, desked and judged, and why each rule is there. Dated entries in the order they were learned; where two differ, the later one stands. Continued in writing-news-2.md.

- Owner's clarification, 2026-10-17: «لماذا يهمّ» is Khazendar's bounded synthesis of the larger economic meaning, not an analyst's attributed recap. `WHY_POLICY` in `editorial.mjs` binds writer, desk, critic and why-only repairs. Its reported premises have source numbers (`why_basis`, retained in run audits, never rendered); the critic checks their meaning and the inference separately. Number checks alone do not prove grounding. Explain standard definitions directly, with the reference in the source list, without «وفق تعريف المرجع». Style improvements carry no «تحديث تحريري» banner; genuine factual corrections still carry their correction notes. Do not mistake synthetic critic calibration or a repaired known article for evidence of autonomous writing quality; use the optional unseen-event writing audit described in README.
- A news story is written in two stages (`write.mjs`): the desk notes first (`deskNotes()`: 8-20 atomic facts of the one event, each tied to its source and checked against it; a fact copied from an Arabic source or carrying figures its source lacks is dropped), then the story from the notes; with notes in hand the writer sees Arabic sources only through the notes, so it cannot copy their sentences. The critic scores from real errors (a changed figure, direction, period, unit, actor, attribution, or an unsupported reported claim; the synthesis box may draw bounded inferences from sourced premises), never from faithful paraphrase, and the second pass is told what was sent for revision. The editor's working headline is dropped when it frames a roundup («أسبوع المصارف المركزية…», «تترقب», «بين … و»).
- Mechanics that keep quality without touching articles (2026-09-13 sweep): the editor cites at most five items per story (`select.mjs` cuts more); translated speech is reported, never quoted in «» (`write.mjs` HOUSE_STYLE); the verbatim-overlap issue names the copied passages so the reviser rewrites them; a first-pass "publish" needs score ≥ 6 and clean checks or it takes the revision round; rejected items get a 12-hour mark, then one more chance, then three weeks; a news story needs a fetched body; a run stops taking stories after 35 minutes; explainers open on the week's news they explain (until 2026-10-23 they stated no current events, see below); Commons licences with NC or ND are refused; the copy-desk guard reads through an attached و/ف/ب/ل/ك and accepts the desk's own number-word grammar and thousands separators; the newsroom commits its state after every run, published or not, and a rebase that cannot apply fails the step instead of passing silently; the calendar computes UTC times per date from the institutions' local time zones.
- Measured against الشرق الأوسط's economy desk on 2026-09-24 (the owner: "can we reach its sophistication level and high quality editorial output even if my website is automated?"): the page design is near parity; the gaps are the news file (their ~30 economy stories a day against our ~7 across all sections, and far more Gulf and Egypt news) and three writing habits now checked in `style.mjs` (an Arab outlet cited for a public fact, a disagreement between sources printed, a large count in raw digits in the headline). A revised draft whose remaining faults are the desk's trade takes one more targeted desk pass instead of being thrown away (`lastDeskPass()` in `run.mjs`); STYLE.md has the detail. `--dry-run --preview=DIR` writes what a run would publish to DIR. The news file, same day with the owner's go (more Claude use accepted): ten Gulf, Egyptian and Moroccan feeds (`regionalNotes` in `sources.json` lists the ones that failed: SPA, WAM, Argaam, Asharq Business…), a daily cap of 20 (was 10), each run takes at least one Gulf or Egypt/Maghreb story until the day holds eight (`ARAB_DAILY`), every feed takes its turn in the editor's list of 300, and price tables are left out (`SERVICE_ITEM`); `feeds.mjs` reads Arabic feed dates and strips Al Riyadh's stray «>» in links. STYLE.md §2 has the detail.
- The newsroom balances sections: a news section with nothing in three days takes its best candidate one point under the threshold in place of the weakest chosen story of a section already served (`run.mjs`, "section balance").
- Reader clarity (2026-10-17): `docs/arabic-editorial-audit-2026-10-17.md` records eight reports from four Arabic desks. No compulsory second/third paragraph, no deletion of useful explanatory repetition, no invented background. Desk notes must select the supplied context and decisive scope qualifications. The existing news critic call records scope/context/flow/precision observations; concrete findings reach revision even beside a high score, and run reports retain both passes. Publication thresholds stay unchanged. `scripts/editorial-eval.mjs --live` is an opt-in critic calibration on synthetic cases, never a publication or proof of writer quality.
- News depth (the owner, 2026-10-18: "i still see low quality articles (also too short) being published"): a news story is a developed report or it is not published. `NEWS_DEPTH`, `NEWS_FLOOR` (300 words, lede plus body; target 350-650) and `NEWS_MIN_FACTS` (12 checked desk notes before a story is written) in `pipeline/lib/editorial.mjs` bind the desk notes (15-30 facts, each tagged news/driver/numbers/reaction/background/next), the writer, the reviser, the copy desk (may not cut under the floor) and the critic (one point and one «ينقص التقرير:» issue per element a source supplies and the draft omits). A draft that passes the second critic pass while still marked "revise" is mended once more from those findings before the precheck (`mendedAfterReview` in the run report; thresholds unchanged). The "a thin event runs as a brief" rule of 2026-09-24 is withdrawn; it had produced 124-193-word stories scored 10. STYLE.md has the detail.
- Short stories (the owner, 2026-10-18: "do renowned news websites publish short stories?", then al-aalem.com's 200-word item as the model, and "i did not like how the summary you built looks like"): an event whose notes hold fewer than `NEWS_MIN_FACTS` facts runs as an ordinary article in short form (`SHORT_STORY`, `SHORT_WORDS` 120-320 words, target 150-250, `SHORT_DAILY_CAP` 7 in 24 hours, all in editorial.mjs): the same writer, copy desk (its floor is the short one), critic (rubric `short`: never asks for depth or boxes), revision, mend and precheck, and the picture desk; no «الأرقام» box, no «لماذا يهمّ», no evidence pair; frontmatter `format: short`. The front never puts one on the cover or among the secondaries (`pickLead()`, index.astro) unless the owner features it; it runs in the digest, sections, الأحدث and the ticker. A separate daily briefs page («موجز خازندار») was built the same day and dropped at his word before it went live. `KHAZENDAR_NEWS_MIN_FACTS` / `KHAZENDAR_SHORT_CAP` override the numbers for a test run.
- Machine habits (the owner, 2026-10-19: "is the arabic ... robotic and scream ai? i want my website to have financial times level of reporting"): the grammar was sound; what gave the machine away was structure. Four habits are cut everywhere and checked in `style.mjs` (`absenceNotes`, `exhortations`, `bridgeOpenings`, the abstract summing-up in `BANNED`): a sentence on what the sources do not say (the house style itself had asked for it), a speech told call by call, abstract summing-up, paragraphs bridged to the one before. Desk notes tag a speaker's general remarks `remark` (two at most kept) and `substantiveFacts()` leaves them out of `NEWS_MIN_FACTS`. STYLE.md §1 has the detail and the calibration (245 published stories: 3 sent back, 4 warned).
- Who and where in the headline (the owner, 2026-10-29, on «أكوا» و«إي آي جي» تؤسسان منصة كهرباء أمريكية برأسمال مستهدف 4 مليارات دولار: "confusing … no keywords for the location", then "fix that at the source, by doing like what famous news outlets do"). The lede said «أكوا» السعودية; the headline and dek, all a front-page card shows, did not. The desks give an actor the reader may not place its country in the headline: «"جي إي فيرنوفا" الأميركية تضخ 9 مليارات دولار…» (الشرق بلومبرغ), «أرباح "البحري" السعودية» (الاقتصادية), Reuters' "Oman's Asyad Group", "Saudi dairy giant Almarai" (all on 28 October); household names and world prices take none. The rule is in the writer's HEADLINES (write.mjs, WHO AND WHERE), the copy desk's headline discipline (it may move the country to the dek's first words, never drop or invent one) and the critic's scope check (editorial.mjs). Code holds the case it can see: `unplacedActors()` (lib/regions.mjs) finds a name the story gives a nationality («X» + an optional Latin gloss + a nisba) that the headline carries while neither it nor the dek names that country or one of its main cities; `styleIssues()` sends it back as the desk's trade. Household names (`HOUSEHOLD`: BP, Shell, Apple, Aramco …) are exempt. Calibrated on all 372 published news stories: 4 hits, each real (ACWA, Scatec, Inscale, SK Hynix); the first version also flagged «رؤى الحرم المكي» under a headline saying «مكة» and «بي بي» beside «مصر», hence the cities and the household list. A headline whose story gives its actor no country at all (Bahri, Talaat Moustafa, Apollo) is left to the writer, desk and critic rules.
- The headline is the spine (the owner, 2026-10-29, on «الفيدرالي الأمريكي يبقي الفائدة بين 3.75% و4% رغم مطالبة عضوتين برفعها»: "check if this article is just a mish-mash/mix of info that look disconnected", then "fix this issue at the source"). Every sentence was sourced (33 checked, none wrong) and the critic scored it 8, yet it read as the Guardian's election piece and Asharq Al-Awsat's markets piece laid side by side: the two dissenters were named in the lede and never again, what explained the split (the statement's «قوية», Warsh's "family fight", Waller, December's pricing) sat in four distant paragraphs, the market paragraph printed two dollar indices as one dollar with three instruments each at its own minute, a paragraph defined interest rates by credit cards and mortgages (the Guardian's line for a general reader), the box printed «10 أصوات» for a 10-2 vote, and «لماذا يهمّ» closed by taking its inference back («من دون أن يحسم وحده اتجاه القرار»). Both critic passes had cleared its flow. Now: `EDITORIAL_CRAFT` (editorial.mjs, read by the writer, the copy desk and the critic) has THE HEADLINE IS THE SPINE (what the headline says is new is developed right after the lede with every supplied fact on it gathered, a speaker's remarks beside what they explain, never a reason no source gives; when the sources cannot develop it, the headline leads on what they can), MARKETS AS A REACTION (one measure per instrument, timed against the event, a retreat from a high said with where the day stands, no market a source does not tie to the event) and no definition of the basic words of economics; `WHY_POLICY` ends the box on its inference; the reader check's flow starts from the headline; the critic's news rubric writes an abandoned headline as «العنوان بلا متن:», a depth defect, and the reviser is shown the sources it names (write.mjs); the desk notes keep every fact that develops the headline's news; `FIGURES_RULE` prints a vote whole. Code: `BOX_TAKEBACK` (style.mjs) warns the desk on a box that says what something does not do «وحده» or closes «دون أن… وحده» (6 of 372 published boxes, each that construction). Two dollar indices (2 stories) and three clock times (2) were measured but left to the critic and reviser: the copy desk's figure guard cannot drop a figure or a «» name, so a warning to it would be noise. Tested by writing the story again from its re-fetched sources through notes, writer, desk, critic and one revision (n=1, harness in that session's scratchpad, fedtest.mjs): the dissent developed in the two paragraphs after the lede, one market sentence «بعد صدور القرار» with one dollar index, no definition, «التصويت: 10 مقابل 2», a box ending on its inference; critic 6 then 9. The first draft had invented a reason for the split («يدور الانقسام حول موعد الزيادة التالية»), caught by the critic; the spine rule now says gather, never explain beyond the sources.
- Figures nothing explains (the owner, 2026-10-30, on «اقتصاد منطقة اليورو ينمو 0.4% في الربع الثالث رغم انكماش فرنسا»: "is this a well written article or just spits information without context?", then "Yes, all three"). Every figure matched Eurostat, but its one readable source was Eurostat's release (the FT and Bloomberg were paywalled one-liners), so it never said why France shrank, what was expected or what it meant: its 300 words were the country table read out row by row and Eurostat's coverage and revision calendar, and both critic passes marked its context clear on comparisons from that same table. Its notes held 25 facts (11 figures, one "driver" that was a backdrop it grew despite, no reaction) and passed `NEWS_MIN_FACTS`. Three changes: (1) `NEWS_DEPTH` and the critic's rubric (verify.mjs) call a statistics office's coverage, method and revision calendar (the next release date excepted) and a table's rows read out in the text padding, and «the numbers in perspective» are the one or two comparisons that change how the figure reads; (2) the reader check's context names which explanations the sources supply (a cause of this change, an expectation met or missed with its figure, a named reaction or consequence), and more figures from the same table count for none; (3) an event whose notes hold fewer than `NEWS_MIN_EXPLAINING` (2) facts tagged driver or reaction runs as a short story (`explainingFacts()` in write.mjs, run.mjs), and the notes now define the tags: a backdrop is background, an outlet's one-word framing («غير متوقع»، «أفضل من المتوقع») is news, a forecast beaten or missed with its figure is a reaction, a statistics office's method and calendar a remark. Notes whose facts mostly carry no job are asked again (two of 83 notes in the cloud logs of 24-30 October had none, which would now read as nothing explaining the event). Calibrated on those 83 tallies (five long stories had one such fact or none) and by taking the notes again for 13 stories near the line (harness in that session's scratchpad, notestest3.mjs): the euro-area story went short on 4 of 4 takes, nine others stayed long on every take (Anthropic's filing, the Egyptian Exchange's halt, Russian diesel, QatarEnergy–Edison, Libya's airspace, Egypt's minimum wage, Saudi utility tariffs, Pakistan's rate rise, SABIC), and Bahri's tanker contract, whose only "causes" are its own stated aims, went short once in two. `KHAZENDAR_NEWS_MIN_EXPLAINING` overrides the bar for a test run. Two faults found the same evening by an independent review: the fact chosen as the notes' `reader_context` lost its job when `prioritizeContext()` removed its listed twin, so a story with exactly two explaining facts counted one and went short (it now keeps the twin's job); and untagged notes were refused on every try, so three refusals would have left the writer with no notes at all, writing long from the raw sources past every depth gate (they are asked again once, then kept, explaining nothing). And one writer fault from the 18:03 cloud run: the Hormuz mine attack (Brent at 117 dollars, the day's lead) was rejected after revision at 5, its critic faulting, among real errors, every price given its own minute (17:56, 17:55, 17:21) and Brent quoted on two contracts. The minutes came from our own instructions: the house style's model figure and the market data's model sentence carry «بحلول الساعة 10:25 بتوقيت غرينتش», while MARKETS AS A REACTION and the critic took the minute away only for a decision, a release or a statement. Writer, market data, editorial craft and critic now say one thing: the clock time is for a markets story, where the price move is the news; a story about any other event (an attack, an outage, a deal too) times its prices against the event; and one future is quoted in one delivery month throughout a story.
- Writing faults, the same night (the owner, on «مجموعة «2 بوينت زيرو» الأبوظبية» and an explainer's dek «لا، فالرقم الأمريكي معدل سنوي…» under «…فهل الفارق بهذا الحجم فعلاً؟»: "weird arabic", then "check for writing bugs and fix them"). Two independent close reads of the 43 pieces of 28-30 October as an Arabic desk editor, every pattern counted over all 445 pieces before a rule was written. Fixed: the who-and-where rule asks for the COUNTRY's adjective, never one coined from a city or an emirate, and `styleIssues()` sends back «الأبوظبي…»، «الظبياني»، «الدبوي» (one hit in 445, the one he found); an explainer's dek is a statement of its own, never a reply (the writer's schema, and `styleIssues()` refuses a dek opening «لا،»، «نعم،»، «الجواب:»…; three explainers in 445, each real); TOLD ONCE in `EDITORIAL_CRAFT` (writer, desk, critic): a fact the dek or lede gave is not given again in other words (ten pieces of 30 October told one figure three or four times; the 8-word check sees only verbatim repeats); «الباحث الأول في» joins the senior-title calque («كبير الباحثين»), «تتجاوز مضيق» (bypass) is a warning («تتفادى»), «كيفن وارش» → «كيفين وارش» (the site's 10 against 4) in the spelling table; the copy desk's examples carry the day's one-off idioms (pro forma «على أساس افتراضي», prepared remarks, cuts both ways, among the lowest on record, data-rich, «النخبوية», «الجرائم المشفرة»…), since a rule for a phrase seen once does not stop the next story's. Measured, already fixed by the rules of 29 October: the «رهن/مرهون» box (12 of the 29 pieces before them, none of the 16 after), the box closing «، لا…»/«وحده» (none on 30 October), bare Latin names in prose (none on 30 October). Left: «ارتفاعاً من» for "up from" (the Arab wires' own usage), a digit 1 or 2 before a counted noun in the box (2 values in 445 pieces).
- Two faults of 31 October (the owner, on Egypt's budget deficit under a crowded Cairo street market, Ataba in 2019 beneath an Infinix phone billboard: "is this image really the best picture for this article? or it was chosen due to a bug?", and on «إكسون وشيفرون الأمريكيتان تتجاوزان التوقعات بأرباح تكرير قياسية مع حرب إيران»: "is it good or makes you feel lost?", then "Yes, fix both"). Neither was a code fault; both were our own rules. (1) **The picture.** `ECONOMY_RULE` and the planner put a budget and the debt beside growth and trade, pictured by "economic life at work", so the planner made Ataba the story's own place, the judge took it as the one picture of the economy at work, and the second check passed it RIGHT. A story about the state's own accounts (`treasuryStory()` in images.mjs, read from the headline alone: a budget, the public debt or its interest, tax revenue, public spending; over 448 pieces the headline found 6, each one, where the dek had also caught a company's «ميزانية», the public debt insurance funds hold and a bond market's fear of a deficit) now has no place tier and no search for a market, a street, a port or a factory (`treasuryQueries()`); a market file never reaches a judge or passes the code (`treasuryFault()`, in `publishedFault()` for the audit too); and the planner, every judge and the check are told such a story is pictured by the country's money photographed as money, or its finance minister when the headline names the minister or the ministry, or runs as text. A story about what households pay (a subsidy, a price or a fee the state sets) keeps its market. Every judge (`GLANCE_RULE`) and the check (`photoRules()`) refuse a frame led by an advertisement for a brand the story does not name. The Ataba file's record says nothing of a market (its one category is «Ataba»), so code alone could not have caught the file: the place tier did. Dry run of the story as new: no place and no market searched, every search for Egyptian money; Commons held scans, Pexels British pounds and Openverse timed out, so it runs as text. (2) **The writing.** The Exxon and Chevron story went back and forth between the companies (Exxon and Chevron, Chevron, Exxon, both, both, then Chevron three times), gave Chevron's whole release and never Exxon's own profit, and called refining by five names («التكرير»، «إنتاج الوقود»، «المصب»، «التكرير والتسويق»، «المشتقات»); both critic passes passed its construction. `EDITORIAL_CRAFT` now has SEVERAL COMPANIES IN ONE STORY (the lede gives the shared finding with one figure for each, then one block of paragraphs per company in the headline's order with the same measures, and a claim about one company shown with both its figures when the sources give them) and ONE NAME FOR ONE THING; the critic's news rubric writes «بناء الشركات:» for paragraphs that go back and forth, «ينقص التقرير:» for a measure given for one company only, and one issue for a thing under two names. Rewritten from its re-fetched sources (notes, writer, desk, critic, one revision; n=1, harness in that session's scratchpad, exxontest.mjs): an Exxon block and a Chevron block under their own subheads, the shared backdrop told once, «التكرير» throughout, critic 6 then 8, its second pass calling the blocks right. No code check for the back and forth: company names in a headline are not reliably readable in code.
- What it does, and a picture that says the point (the owner, 2026-11-01, on «أرامكو تكمل الاستحواذ على 49% من «هيوماين» السعودية…»: the photograph "does not give a hint" of HUMAIN, and the headline and dek "do not give any hint to what type of company is هيوماين"; and on a date market under «انكماش الاقتصاد السعودي يتباطأ إلى 3.8% في الربع الثالث»: "not the best choice in my opinion"; "if these are bugs fix them"). Three were our own rules, one a bug. (1) **Trade in the headline**: the who-and-where rule asked for a company's country, never what it does, where Reuters had headlined this very deal "Saudi Aramco to take minority stake in PIF-owned AI firm Humain" (2025-10-28). The writer, the copy desk and the critic now ask for the trade the sources give when a name does not say it («هيوماين» السعودية للذكاء الاصطناعي), in the headline or the dek's first words, and `untradedActors()` (lib/regions.mjs, read by `styleIssues()`) sends back a headline carrying a name its own story describes («X» للذكاء الاصطناعي، «X» المتخصصة في …، شركة الذكاء الاصطناعي «X») with no word of that in the headline or dek: a household name is exempt (`HOUSEHOLD`, now with the rating agencies and OpenAI), and a purpose («للحصول على») or a facility («خط «شرق - غرب» للخام») is no trade. Over 405 news stories: 6 hits, each real (HUMAIN, Arada, Rua Al Haram, Binance, Solidigm, DeepSeek). (2) **The brand pass took the household name**: the planner's `company` was Aramco, the buyer; for one deal between two companies it now names the one a reader is less likely to know. No free photograph of HUMAIN exists (Commons holds its logo as a drawn SVG only; the outlets ran AFP's photograph of its booth), so the story still runs a data-centre illustration, which the headline's trade now explains. (3) **The date market** came from `ECONOMY_RULE` and the planner: a whole economy was pictured by "an everyday market with its shoppers", and oil counted as "one commodity it mentions in passing" though the dek named it as the driver («…بقيادة الأنشطة النفطية»). Now the sector the headline or standfirst says moved the figure comes first (Saudi output led by oil: its terminals, tankers and refineries); an everyday market belongs to what households pay or spend, not to output or growth (this narrows the planner's places rule of 25 October, under which the Shorja market ran with Iraq's IMF request); and a market of one traditional product (dates, spices, incense, carpets) is folklore, refused in code (`NOVELTIES`) unless the story is about that product. (4) **The bug**: an amateur frame held back as the last resort is read again with the standard set aside, and an answer of RECAPTION (the frame fits, its caption names too much) dropped it where the main path rewrites the caption: the Jubail refinery under the GDP story was lost that way, and the story would have run as text. Dry runs as new: the HUMAIN story's planner named HUMAIN and searched its brand (nothing free); the GDP story searched only Saudi oil. Self-tests pin each change and fail on the old code. The published stories were not re-picked (his ruling of 28 October).
