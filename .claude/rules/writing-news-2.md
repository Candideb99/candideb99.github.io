# Writing news: names, cards, construction, joined prose (November 2026)

Continues writing-news-1.md. Dated entries in the order they were learned; where two differ, the later one stands.

- Codex's fifth review, the same day ("codex reported these"), five writing faults, all real; per the owner's rule of 2026-10-28 the published stories were left as they are and each fault traced to its step. (1) **The copy desk could not fix a dual's case**: its guard read «شركتان» and «شركتين» as two facts and refused «مع شركتان» → «مع شركتين» as a changed number; `dualsOf()` (copydesk.mjs) reads a dual whatever its case, and dropping the count is still refused. (2) **«هذه الابتكار», «ملتزمين»** in the ECB story of 21 September, desked on the 23rd by the desk's first version; the archive holds no other such slip («هذه السوق» is right), so nothing was added. (3) **«لكن هذا الرقم» after Brent's close** in the Saudi growth explainer of 1 November, where the contraction was meant: `styleIssues()` warns the desk when «هذا الرقم/هذه النسبة…» opens a sentence after two sentences that each give a figure (over the 469 pieces it fired there alone). (4) **A watt per hour for a watt-hour** («كيلووات/ساعة»، «للميغاواط/ساعة», 14 times in 5 stories against 22 right): the house spelling table writes «… ساعة» without the slash. (5) **A launch story ran its car's range and charging under a subhead about factory capacity, at the end**: THE HEADLINE IS THE SPINE (editorial.mjs) now says a launch is developed by the thing launched right after the lede and a subhead names what its own paragraphs say. A prompt rule, not yet seen in a story the writer has written under it.
- A woman by a man's name (the owner, 2026-11-01, on «وقالت يوسف» in the industrial-permits story: "treating a masculine name as feminine… is this because claude is using english language as its thinking language? or is this a bug?"). Neither: Youssef is Nahed Youssef, the woman who heads the Industrial Development Authority, introduced in the story's first paragraph, and the verb agrees with her. What reads wrong is her family name alone, a man's given name: the English wires' "Youssef said" (its English source, Daily News Egypt, wrote "Youssef added"; its Arabic one, البورصة, «وأوضحت ناهد يوسف»), and no rule said how the Arabic press names people on later mentions. The writer's house style (LATER MENTIONS, write.mjs) and the copy desk now name such a woman by her full name or her title every time («وقالت ناهد يوسف»، «وأضافت رئيسة الهيئة»); a family name that reads as one may stand alone («المشاط»، «مدبولي»). `womenBySurname()` (style.mjs) sends back a feminine verb, or «بحسب/حسب/وفق» before a comma or a particle, followed by such a name alone after her full name, and a feminine verb before such a name standing alone; a first name never takes the article or is a man's name, so «قالت الهيئة إن محمد يوسف…» marks no one. Over the 472 published pieces it found exactly the two that did it (Nahed Youssef three times, Mary Salem's «بحسب سالم، التي حذرت» once). The published stories were left as they are (his ruling of 2026-10-28).
- One person on the card, 2026-11-07 (the owner, on «رئيس فيدرالي نيويورك: التضخم مرتفع للغاية ويجب إعادته إلى 2%» over the dek «تصريحات جون ويليامز في فرانكفورت…»: "who is john williams? is this sub-optimal news writing? or leaving john williams as it is makes the reader curious to read the article?"). The story's own lede had it right («قال رئيس الاحتياطي الفيدرالي في نيويورك جون ويليامز…»). Now the writer (ONE PERSON ON THE CARD, write.mjs), the copy desk and the critic's card check (editorial.mjs) keep a person the headline gives by title alone either unnamed in the dek or named there with the headline's title beside the name, and `bareNameInDek()` (style.mjs, read by `styleIssues()`) sends back a dek naming alone a person the headline gives only by title: a word is a person's family name when the story uses it alone after a verb of saying («وتحدث ويليامز»، «وقال رايت»), and its title before or after it in the dek clears it; a name with the article («الجدعان») is left to the prompts, since «وقال البنك»، «وذكر التقرير» read the same. Over the 472 published news stories it finds exactly this one and «وزير الطاقة الأمريكي: …» over «رايت: …». `CHECKER_VERSION` is unchanged (8abd70ec7f). `scripts/pipeline-selftest.mjs` pins it (its first check fails on the old code). His photograph is no fault: the free libraries hold none since 2018, and a search for his name finds the film composer, so the story ran an illustration of dollars.

- How the pieces are built, 2026-11-11 (the owner: "check some new articles and writings on the website and check if their arabic and journalism style is good or needs improving by improving the writer (control room? rules? logic?)"). Read as a desk editor: the 26 newest pieces of every kind (15 long news stories, 3 short, 3 explainers, 2 analyses, 2 paper readings, the weekly), every pattern counted over all 592 before a rule. The words were sound: the house checks found fifteen things in the newest 45 pieces, fourteen of them light warnings, and the idiom slips were one-offs («توجيهاتها الأولية» for a company's guidance, «المدير الإداري الأول», «يسخن أكثر من اللازم», «بوتيرة صحية», a bare «EBITDA» as an Arabic noun: each in one to four pieces, now among the copy desk's before → after examples). What read as a machine was construction, each a limit a brief already stated and no code held. (1) **News chopped under subheads**: the schema allowed "one or two if the piece is long", and the long stories went from 0.67 subheads each before 18 October to 2.2 since 1 November (11 of 100 with four or more, 27 with a subhead over one short paragraph, a 299-word story under four). The cause was my rule of 1 November that a paragraph not matching its subhead "moves to where it belongs": the reviser gave it a subhead of its own. THE HEADLINE IS THE SPINE and the writer's schema now say a subhead heads a section of two paragraphs or more, about one for every 200 words and three at most. (2) **A document read out item by item**: the Bank of Japan's summary of opinions (a source added on 9 November) became fifteen sentences of «جاء في أحد الآراء… وذهب رأي آخر… ودعا رأي ثالث»; one story of 592, but every summary or set of minutes would read so: the machine-habits line of `EDITORIAL_CRAFT` (writer, desk, critic) says such items are grouped by what they say. (3) **Analyses that tour the week**: the analysis editor was told to pick a theme "where at least two of the stories connect" from two to five stories, and 4 of the 16 analyses since 23 October ran four or five subheads against the schema's three, each a tour (Saudi ports, then the Suez Canal, Hormuz and the Gulf railway; Egypt's gas cut, then the reserves and a gas discovery; five companies in five sections). The editor now picks one question a story leaves, with at most four stories (`ANALYSIS_STORIES`, select.mjs: the one it comes from and those whose facts the answer needs); the writer is told ONE ARGUMENT, the material is evidence and not a list to cover, a lede under 60 words; the news rule that a paragraph never links back, which the analyst also read, is said of news only (an analysis, like an explainer, is a line of reasoning; the desk too); the critic writes «خارج الحجة:». (4) **Explainers coining terms**: under the brief of 8 November (the field's terms, each with its English, a glossary of three to five) both explainers since carried an everyday idea dressed as a term with an English gloss (a comparison with last year, a decision about one more unit). A term is now one a first textbook indexes, an idea without such a name stays in plain words, and the glossary holds the two to five terms really taught; the critic writes «مصطلح مصطنع:». (5) **The weekly as a figure dump**: 986 words and 95 figures under «أبرز ما جرى» on 6 November (62 and 52 figures in the two before), each story told again in 60-110 words with its "key figures". Each now gets 40-70 words on what happened and the two or three figures it turns on (`weeklyParagraphFault()`, 30-95 words held in code). Code: `structureFaults()` (style.mjs) reports a news story's subheads (more than one for 150 words or more than three, or a section under 60 words), a document's items (five or more «رأي آخر/ثالث…»), an analysis with more than three subheads or a lede past 75 words, as warnings that open «البناء:»; run.mjs hands them to the revision a story is getting anyway (news) or to the analysis's revision and mend, as an explainer's off-plan figures go; none rejects a piece, and the copy desk, whose guard refuses a changed subhead count, never sees them (`WRITER_ONLY`). An analysis dek that sets a second thing beside the first with «، أما … فـ» is sent back as «فيما/بينما» is (2 of 29, one written two days after that rule; «لكن» deks state one finding and stay). Over the archive: 38 of the 100 long news stories since 1 November carry a subhead finding (30 of 228 before 18 October), the Bank of Japan story alone the items finding, 4 of 16 analyses the subheads and 5 of 29 the lede. Self-tests pin each (they fail on the old checks). Nothing was run through the newsroom: the prompt changes have not yet been seen in a published piece (the next analysis and explainer are the day's, the next weekly Friday's). Looked at and left: figure tables still read out in release stories (Germany's trade of 9 November, a third of its body rows of Destatis's table; about one story in ten has two such paragraphs, and the rule of 30 October already names it: the desk notes' tallies of 61 stories did not separate them, numbers-heavy notes also made sound stories, so no routing was changed; the open question of 29 October stands, whether a story found thin after writing should fall to the short form); the writer's system prompt, 47,000 characters of rules with their histories, where the explainers improved when their brief was cut to a plan and 4,000 characters; a paper reading's lede carrying all four authors with their Latin names (80 words, by the rule of 24 October); two Arabic names for EBITDA's parts (9 pieces «الإهلاك والاستهلاك», 3 «الإهلاك والإطفاء», 1 «الاستهلاك والإطفاء»); an official quoted three times by title and never named (the Suez Canal story; a rough search found two more in 506); a subject-first «فلان، منصبه، قال» paragraph (4 of 506).
- A table read out, 2026-11-12 (the question left open on 29 October and 11 November: should a story found thin after it is written run short?; the owner's "Yes, build A"). A news story whose only readable source is a statistics release or a results statement filled its 300-word floor with the release's rows (Germany's trade of 9 November: 39% of its body Destatis's table, and a lede ending on a method note), and NEWS_DEPTH named this since 30 October without holding it: the German story's notes passed `NEWS_MIN_EXPLAINING`, and the desk-note tallies of 61 stories could not tell such stories from sound ones, so the check reads the written text. `tableReadout()` (style.mjs): a paragraph of five figures or more (a year and the day of a month are not figures) with no cause word and no speaker is a row read out; two such paragraphs making a quarter of the body or more are caught, a warning that opens `READOUT` («البناء: المتن يسرد صفوف جدول»), handed to the writer only (`WRITER_ONLY`). Calibrated on the 488 long news stories published to 11 November: 36 had two such paragraphs, 24 (4.9%) at a quarter or more; the eight since 25 October were each a read-out on reading (Germany's and Morocco's trade, Egypt's and Saudi Arabia's budgets, euro-area GDP, the results of Elm, IHC and FAB), those just under the bar (Emaar, Egypt's inflation, the Fed, 13-24%) sound; no analysis, explainer or reading trips it. The finding rides with the revision a story is getting anyway, and code decides its form (`readoutRoute()`): `wordsWithoutRows()` estimates the story with its rows given up for two or three sentences (`READOUT_SUMMARY_WORDS`, 40); under the floor, and while the day has room under `SHORT_DAILY_CAP` and the story is no refresh, it is revised as a short story keeping its chart and table, where the rows belong (`goShort()`, `entry.shortAfterReadout`); at or over the floor it is revised long with the rows cut; past the cap a finding that would take the story under the floor is withheld, and the story is revised as before. Since 25 October that sends 3 of the 8 short (Germany, Elm, Egypt's budget) and keeps 5 long. The mend, which runs when the second critic still says "revise", carries the finding the same way, because a dry run showed the rows coming back after the first draft: the German story written again from its re-fetched sources (14 Claude calls) had one row paragraph, 18%, in its first draft, and two, 26% of 318 words, after the revision merged a thin section and the critic asked for "the trend from the table"; a short mend becomes the story only once it passes its checks, and goes out without a further critic pass, as every mend does. The first build let the reviser choose its form; given the choice it padded to the floor both times, at 309 words on the dry run's caught draft and at 301 on the published story, the second with Destatis's method note put back, so the choice is code's. Under the final route the published story became a 181-word short story with its chart and table and no row paragraph left caught (one call; nothing published). Cost: no extra Claude call; the short form's own revision and critic replace the long ones. `scripts/pipeline-selftest.mjs` pins the check, its limits and the route (they fail on the old code, which has none of them). Not yet seen in a published story. The test harnesses are in that session's scratchpad (readout-dry.mjs runs one published story through the real `produceStory()` as a dry run; readout-short.mjs the route's revision alone).
- Joined prose (الوصل), 2026-11-12 (the owner: "did the news writing style (eloquence, high quality arabic language) has improved in latest published news … or it still needs improvements? if yes, then improve"). First: the rules of 11 and 12 November (construction, the table read-out) and the control-room fixes of 10 November had never been committed or pushed, so the 24 pieces published from 10 November evening to 12 November were written under the old rules; run over them, the unpublished checks find a subhead fault in 8 of the 19 long news stories (Abu Dhabi Ports: six subheads in 611 words), a table read out in one and a touring analysis. Read as a desk editor, the grammar and vocabulary are sound and the idiom slips one-offs (2-4 pieces of 616 each: a company's «توجيهات» for guidance, «قريباً نسبياً», «X من المقرر أن يُعقد», a measure's long name five times in one results story, «إجمالي صافي ربح», share counts in nine raw digits; all but the last now among the copy desk's examples). The one new systematic fault is the prose's joints: paragraphs opened bare, one statement after another, the English way (the German advisers' story: nine in a row, «المجلس…»، «المستشار الألماني فريدريش ميرتس قال…»، «تخطط الحكومة…»). It was our rule: «paragraphs open on their own actor, figure or date» (19 October, written against filler bridges) was obeyed by dropping the «و» too, and a warning told the desk that paragraphs opening «و» + verb were a fault. Measured: follow-on paragraphs with no connective were 10% of our long news before 19 October, 25% in the two weeks after, 17% since; in 16 stories of Asharq Al-Awsat's and Anadolu's economy desks read that day with the newsroom's own reader, 7-9%, their commonest openers «وفي»، «وقال»، «وأوضح»، «وأضاف», and 4 of the 16 tripped our old «و» warning. Now the writer's house style (ARABIC PROSE IS JOINED, write.mjs), the copy desk and the machine-habits line of `EDITORIAL_CRAFT` say a paragraph that carries on opens with «و» and its verb or «لكن»، «أما … فـ», a bare opening is for the first paragraph, the first under a subhead and a real turn, the verb of saying comes before its subject, and only the filler bridge is cut. Code (style.mjs): `unjoinedParagraphs()` warns the desk when half or more of a news story's follow-on paragraphs (four or more) open bare: 6% of the news before 19 October, 20% of 19-31 October, 14% since, none of the 16 desk stories; `subjectFirstSpeech()` warns on «الفاعل … قال» opening a sentence (2 stories of 527, both real); the old «و» warning fires only above 85% of five paragraphs or more (none of 527). Both are ordinary warnings, so the copy desk mends them (adding «و» changes no figure). `scripts/pipeline-selftest.mjs` pins them (they fail on the old code, which has neither). Not yet seen in a published story; measure again with the session's scratchpad harness (measure.mjs, calib.mjs) after a few days of news.
