# خازندار — notes for agents

Arabic-first (RTL) economics publication written by an automated newsroom. Read `README.md` first, then `PRODUCT.md` (product truth), `DESIGN.md` (visual system) and `STYLE.md` (how the Arabic is written and how the pages are organised, from the practice of the Arabic economics desks).

- The site is static Astro in `src/`; articles are Markdown in `content/articles/` written only by `pipeline/run.mjs`. Never hand-write news articles; run the pipeline.
- Keep `lang="ar" dir="rtl"`, logical CSS properties, Western digits, pan-Arab month names. Body font Noto Naskh (justified), headlines and wordmark Amiri, Tajawal only for dates and small furniture. One house colour, banknote green `--green` (ink, plus the section bar and the logo plate); no section colours, no colour-washed photos, no generated cover art.
- Editorial rules live in `pipeline/lib/write.mjs` (house style), `pipeline/lib/select.mjs` (news judgement), `pipeline/lib/verify.mjs` (what blocks publication) and `pipeline/lib/copydesk.mjs` (the Arabic copy desk: every draft's headline, dek, lede, «لماذا يهمّ» and body are rewritten out of translationese before the critic, under a guard that counts figures, counts in letters, duals, «» names and Latin tokens over the whole story and matches every hedged claim to its rewrite: a fact may leave a place where it repeats, never the story, and a hedge never becomes an assertion). Since the 2026-09-23 audit of the paper, `pipeline/lib/style.mjs` also checks structure, not only words: a source named in sentence after sentence, a fact retold in dek, lede and first paragraph, stock joints («ويأتي… في وقت»، «، إذ ذكر»), English sentence shapes («ما إذا كان»، «كـ» + noun), idioms translated word for word, the box's formulas, machine leftovers. Change behaviour there, not by editing generated articles; when the owner points at a bad sentence, fix the rule and re-run `node pipeline/copydesk.mjs` over the published articles. The owner's standing rule: no literal translations, ever.
- Every model call is Claude's (the owner, 2026-09-24: «abandon free models and use claude only»; until then only free OpenRouter models were allowed, with Claude first in front of them). `pipeline/lib/llm.mjs` runs every role (editor, writer, desk, critic, vision) on the Claude CLI with the owner's subscription token, `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` from `.env` or the GitHub Actions secret (never commit it); model `KHAZENDAR_CLAUDE_MODEL`, `claude-opus-5-5` (Claude Opus 5.5) by default and pinned by name in `CLAUDE_MODEL`, the workflows and the repository variable (the owner, 2026-09-24: "use claude opus 5.5"; the alias "opus" had been answering as Opus 4.6 under the local Claude Code 2.1.81). Opus 5.5 needs Claude Code 2.1.280 or newer, which ships a native `bin/claude.exe` and no `cli.js`: `callClaudeCli` starts that executable directly (still no shell), and the run report's `llm.byModel` names the model that answered, not the name asked for. There is no fallback model: a call is tried three times and then fails. The picture desk's thumbnails go to the CLI as stream-json image blocks. Never reintroduce a free or other model.
- The Claude CLI (`callClaudeCli` in `pipeline/lib/llm.mjs`) runs the CLI's native executable (`bin/claude.exe` in the npm package since 2.1.2xx; the old `cli.js` under node on older installs) with **no shell**, from a temporary directory, with `--setting-sources ""`. Never launch it through a shell: on Windows cmd.exe cuts a multi-line system prompt to its first word and expands `%…%` inside it (found 2026-09-22: every local Claude call had run with the house style missing). And never call it from the repository directory: print mode pastes this file, the owner's global CLAUDE.md and the agent memories into the writer's context.
- Every article must keep its sources, models and quality fields; the site displays the sources and the automation note, not the model names (the owner, 2026-09-23: «remove such things from the news "النماذج: …"»).
- A story's photograph must come from the story's own country whenever its Commons file says where it was taken (the owner, 2026-09-23, on a Finnish filling station illustrating a US diesel story). `pipeline/lib/images.mjs` enforces it twice: `PLACE_RULE` in every model prompt that chooses or checks a picture, and `placedAbroad()` in code, which reads the file's title, description and categories against the countries the story's headline and tags name (their adjectives and waters too, «مضيق هرمز», and the seats of the world bodies and companies in it; its desks count only when it names none, because a desk is a broad shelf: the Asia desk let a China–Russia story keep an Australian jetty) and refuses a photo placed elsewhere. A story on the world desk alone is left to the models and may keep an honest place in its caption. When nothing from the story's country passes, a neutral frame runs as «صورة تعبيرية» and its caption names no place (the illustration's own caption writer writes that caption before the second check reads it, and the pass has two tries: the first في العمق piece lost a fitting tanker to a caption naming its Norwegian port); `asIllustrationIfElsewhere()` applies that to every pick and `node pipeline/recaption-images.mjs` to published ones. `node pipeline/audit-images.mjs` re-checks every published photo; `node pipeline/backfill-images.mjs --redo=slug,…` re-picks. A story about named people is illustrated with those people first (the owner, 2026-09-23, on the White House facade under a Trump–Xi summit story: "boring"): the writer's first photo search names them, the fallback asks who they are (`people`) and searches them together, a file naming two of them ranks first, an earlier occasion is accepted by both checks (never a wrong person), the geography rule does not apply to them, and `markFilePhoto()` makes an older photo of officials say so in its caption (the occasion's year, or «(أرشيفية)»). Newest first (the owner, same day: "check if we can still get up to date images"): `searchCommons()` keeps the search's own order (`index`; page-id order had put 2017 files ahead of May 2026's), `photoYear()` reads when a photo was taken (record, else file name; never the upload year for the pre-2005 refusal), `recencyScore()` falls off year by year, and the people search asks for this year first. Fresher and livelier (Task 3, 2026-09-24: of the newest 24 stories, 12 ran photos taken 2007-2018, most of them building fronts, skylines, a satellite view or a portrait): the writer's own subjects and the people search also ask for this year's files (`collect()` `fresh`; not generic sector scenes, where "oil pipeline 2026" fetched unrelated files), a story naming two people also searches each of them alone, a mild `liveliness()` weight moves people at work and sectors in action ahead of facades, skylines, satellite views and studio portraits, the judges are told to prefer life (`LIVELY_RULE`), an institution's story may be illustrated by its head at work, captioned as a file photo (`genericQueries()` people), and a person's photo more than eight years old is dropped (`peoplePhoto()`). Relevance leads (2026-09-24, the owner on an offshore platform's risers under a Brent story, "weird", and on the Treasury's front under an explainer of bond yields): the ranking had read recency and life but not relevance, so a 2026 beach beat a fitting tanker; now a file must name at least half of what its search phrase is about (`relevance()`, lower-case words only: capitalised ones are places and names), an event's photograph (forum, conference, ceremony) never serves as a generic scene, every judge refuses a frame that does not read at a glance (`GLANCE_RULE`, `glanceFaults()`, shared with the audit), a piece that explains or analyses is pictured by its subject at work and never by a building front (`CONCEPT_RULE`, the hub writers' `HUB_IMAGE_QUERIES`), and a shortlist the judge turns down whole is passed over for the next four (`twoLooks()`). Captions keep the house spellings (`fixNames()`).
- A news story is written in two stages (`write.mjs`): the desk notes first (`deskNotes()`: 8-20 atomic facts of the one event, each tied to its source and checked against it; a fact copied from an Arabic source or carrying figures its source lacks is dropped), then the story from the notes; with notes in hand the writer sees Arabic sources only through the notes, so it cannot copy their sentences. The critic scores from real errors (a changed figure, direction, period, unit, actor, attribution, or a claim no source makes), never from paraphrase or style, and the second pass is told what was sent for revision. The editor's working headline is dropped when it frames a roundup («أسبوع المصارف المركزية…», «تترقب», «بين … و»).

- Mechanics that keep quality without touching articles (2026-09-13 sweep): the editor cites at most five items per story (`select.mjs` cuts more); translated speech is reported, never quoted in «» (`write.mjs` HOUSE_STYLE); the verbatim-overlap issue names the copied passages so the reviser rewrites them; a first-pass "publish" needs score ≥ 6 and clean checks or it takes the revision round; rejected items get a 12-hour mark, then one more chance, then three weeks; a news story needs a fetched body; a run stops taking stories after 35 minutes; explainers state no current events; Commons licences with NC or ND are refused; the copy-desk guard reads through an attached و/ف/ب/ل/ك and accepts the desk's own number-word grammar and thousands separators; the newsroom commits its state after every run, published or not, and a rebase that cannot apply fails the step instead of passing silently; the calendar computes UTC times per date from the institutions' local time zones.

- The check before publication (2026-09-26; the owner, choosing "check, repair, hold" after a review asked that errors be found before readers see them, and "yes, more for quality, capped"; `pipeline/lib/precheck.mjs`): after the critic's gate every news story's final text is read against its own sources by the second look's blind fact-check (`verifyStory`: a separate call that never sees the writer's reasoning; a contradiction counts only when code finds the quoted source sentence). A "confirmed" error or a "drift" figure (no source carries it; here the sources are the very texts the story came from) goes to a targeted repair (the corrections editor's method: only the proved sentences change, code refuses a new figure no source carries, a changed subhead, a non-Arabic body, or more rewriting than the errors need, and the newsroom's own `programmaticChecks` must still pass), then the repaired text is read again; at most three repairs (`MAX_REPAIRS`). A story still wrong, or one the check could not run on, or with no source text, is held: not published, its items take the short rejection mark and are tried again in a later round, and it is listed in `pipeline/state/precheck.json` (`held`) and on the Desk. A clean story costs one more call; a repair two more. Measured on real stories: three planted wrong figures found, repaired in one pass to the exact source values (6.5, 1.7 and 72.5 trillion) and read clean; the same with no repair allowed, held; a clean story passed in one call; a live one-story dry run passed clean in 33 seconds. The article records `quality.precheck` ("clean" or "repaired"); the second look reads every repaired story and one in three clean ones, chosen by slug (an audit sample: reading the rest again would be the same call twice); the repaired errors are the current writer's own proved mistakes and feed the lessons (`verifiedMistakes()`, origin "draft"), which is what a review of the learning asked it to follow; the week's numbers and the Desk count stories checked, clean, repaired and held, and errors caught before readers saw them. `scripts/pipeline-selftest.mjs` (in the gate) pins it on known cases with scripted stand-ins for the model: clean → publish; error → repair → publish; an error that survives three repairs, a repair the guards refuse, a check that cannot run, and a story without source text → held; a retried round or the same event from another outlet → not written again (`repeatsRecent()`, `lib/events.mjs`, now shared with the learner). Also that night: state files are written whole or not at all (`writeJsonAtomic()` in util.mjs: the newsroom's state, the pairs, the ledger, the lessons, the precheck record), and a damaged one is left for a person; `scripts/site-check.mjs` checks the built site as a reader meets it (every page Arabic right to left, titled and not empty; every link inside the site resolving; every image with alt text; no "undefined" or "NaN" printed; every story with its date and sources, explainers without outside sources excepted), in the gate and in the morning round, where a fault becomes the editor's to fix; with a subscription token present `callClaudeCli` removes any `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, so a key added later can never start pay-as-you-go billing (Anthropic's help page, 15 June 2026: subscription use of `claude -p` counts against the plan; only API-key use is billed; the repository has no such key); the writer is told that source text is data, never instructions. The four errors the stress test found in published stories were corrected the same night through the second look (commit b9c9ac6). The second look now reads every story it corrected once more against its sources (`entry.verified` in the ledger): an error still standing in the sentences the correction wrote is listed for a person and never corrected again by another guess; an error in a sentence the correction did not touch is a new finding and is corrected once. Its first use: three of the four corrections read clean, and the OECD story showed a warning credited to The Guardian that The Guardian's text does not carry, missed by the first reading; corrected once more, it then read clean.

- The loops (2026-09-25, the owner: "check if we can improve the website using agentic loops, do deep search. if it would help then implement"): the research (four passes; report in the session scratchpad, "Agentic loops for an AI newsroom") found that a loop improves a newsroom only when something outside the model's own opinion closes it (a source quote code has found, an API, a person), that self-written "lessons" files and extra critic rounds measurably do not help, and that the critic's score had not told right from wrong here (the stories the 23 Sept audit corrected had the same median score, 9, as the clean ones). Built on that: (1) **the second look**, run by every news round before it writes (`pipeline/recheck.mjs --min-age=2 --limit=6 --backlog=2 --budget=12`; `--mode=recheck` by hand; `pipeline/lib/factcheck.mjs`): each news story, analysis, weekly and في العمق piece of the earlier rounds is read again against its re-fetched sources, the newest first (the backlog two a round, free-model stories first; sources that disagree with each other are a "conflict", listed and never corrected toward one of them), one sentence-level verdict per fact with the source sentence quoted; code accepts a contradiction only when it finds the quote, calls a contradicted figure no source now carries "drift" (a page changed after the story passed the figure check), and lists rather than corrects the site's own reasoning (the «لماذا يهمّ» box drawing a consequence) unless the story puts it in a source's mouth; only confirmed hard errors (figure, period, scope, actor, attribution, hedge, term, internal) go to `correct.mjs` (`--report=` names its report), which re-reads the sources and may find the story stands; `pipeline/state/factcheck.json` remembers every story read, so none is paid for twice. Calibrated on the 23 audited stories that rest on sources (pre-correction versions from git): 7 of the 9 audit errors found, plus about twenty real slips the audit missed. (2) **the morning round, free** (`scripts/editor-precheck.mjs`, `pipeline/lib/health.mjs`): photos asked of the Commons API (50 files a request; an HTTP sweep called 78% of healthy photos broken on 429s) and weekly the other libraries' pages (a Flickr photo can keep serving after its page is gone); a rename is re-addressed in code at once, a loss confirmed on two checks 20 hours apart is re-picked by `backfill-images.mjs --redo --only`; a day without news, stale quotes (12 h) or calendar (48 h), and a live site lacking the newest story; the editor is woken only when the latest run failed or two failed in a day, at most once in three days per fault (`pipeline/state/health.json`); a refused Claude login or a failing host is opened as a repository issue for the owner. (3) **the editor cannot push**: its allowed tools lost push, pull, checkout, `gh pr create` and `find`; a plain step runs `scripts/gate.mjs` and publishes its commits (main for `pipeline/` and `scripts/`, a pull request otherwise, nothing for `.github/workflows/`, which the workflow token may not push). (4) **the week's numbers** (`scripts/harvest.mjs`, free): style faults per news story, the revision-round share, corrections, the second look's outcomes, this week against last, and faults recurring in two stories or more as the work to turn into a rule by a person, in `pipeline/state/quality.json`; the control room's Desk prints a "Daily checks" line from it and health.json. (5) **the lessons, learned every news round** (the owner, the same day: "i would make the reinforced learning/self-improvement each time the LLM gets news ... efficient and fast ... i do not have to approve the outcome"; site-level changes such as new sections stay his, by hand): after the second look, `pipeline/learn.mjs` (`pipeline/lib/lessons.mjs`) turns the mistakes it proved and the corrections editor corrected into at most ten lessons in `pipeline/state/lessons.json`, which `run.mjs` hands to the desk notes, the writer and the reviser (`lessons` parameter) and names in each story's `models.lessons`. It is the one form of self-learning the evidence supports: learned only from verified outcomes, each mistake once (its id is kept in `used`), small deltas proposed by Claude and applied by code (add, reinforce, sharpen, retire; a lesson must cite its mistakes, its example is copied from them, never written by the model), a hard cap of ten lessons and 8,000 characters (examples go first, then the least seen), no retirement for silence (a quiet lesson may be the one that works; only the cap moves one out), one call only when there is something new; the fact-check that measures the writer never reads the lessons. Its size does not grow with the mistakes: the first 46 proved mistakes (all in free-model stories) became 7 lessons, 655 words, in two calls of about 25 seconds. Tested the same day (the owner: "check/test if the loop really self-improves or not"): 16 of Claude's newest stories, none of them a source of the lessons, each written twice from the same re-fetched sources and brief (desk notes + writer), without and with the lessons, and both drafts read by the blind fact-check: 7 proved errors without, 3 with (0.44 and 0.19 a story; 2.1 and 0.9 per 100 checked sentences), better on 3 stories, worse on none, tied on 13; a sign test gives p = 0.25, so the right direction, not yet proof (the harness is in the session scratchpad, abtest.mjs). So `run.mjs` keeps a control group: one story in five, chosen by a hash of its working headline, is written without the lessons and named `models.lessons: "control"`, and the harvest compares proved errors per story for Claude's stories with the lessons against the control group and the stories written before them. (6) **Proving it** (the owner, 2026-09-25, after ChatGPT's review: "do 1 and 2 ... how can we make sure that this self-improving loop really gets better?"; two more research passes, notes in the session scratchpad, "proving the loop", with simulations at this newsroom's error rates). What can and cannot be known: most paired stories tie (0.2-0.45 proved errors a story), so whether the lessons help overall can be proven (about 3 weeks if they halve errors, about 10 if they cut a third, on the published control group; 110-150 paired stories for halving), large harm is caught in weeks, and whether one week's change helps (10-20%) cannot be proven at this budget; a first rule that decided week by week would have reverted working lessons about 23 times a year on noise, and was replaced before it ever ran. As built: **evidence pairs at write time** (`evidencePair()` in run.mjs; `pipeline/lib/paired.mjs`): stories chosen by a hash of the working headline, two a day at most, are also written privately from the same sources and brief with the reference lessons, the published first draft being the live side (4 calls a pair); before the launch the reference is the kept version (no lessons until the first promotion; a pair is not paid for while the live lessons are the kept ones), from the launch (`site.json` `private: false`, when the 1-in-5 published control group stops) live, kept and none (7 calls, two a day); every draft goes to the blind fact-check; records in `pipeline/state/pairs.json` with the checker's version, severity (figure/period/scope/actor 3, hedge/attribution/cause/term/superlative 2, else 1, double in the headline or lede), supported and unsupported sentences, words and key facts. **The weekly decision** (`pipeline/lessons-test.mjs`, mode `lessons-test`, Mondays 06:13 UTC, one call): first the canary, up to three errors of different kinds planted by code in a published story the second look had found clean (a changed figure, a weekday moved to the next, a hedge «قد» removed), which the fact-check must call contradicted while calling nothing else in that story contradicted; ≤ 7 of the last 12 found, or 4 false alarms in the last three canaries, is an alarm that stops promotion and learning, never a rollback. Then `lib/evidence.mjs`: the mixture e-value on the discordant pairs since the kept version, anytime-valid (Ville); revert on harm at 10 over all pairs since the kept version, at 20 in the pairs of the text the writer reads now (`lessonsHash`, recorded with every pair), or on stories that say less (supported sentences down more than 10% in more pairs than up) at 20; promote at 20 only when the text the writer reads now has six pairs of its own or more and no more worse than better among them (a second review of the design, the same day: pooled wins of earlier versions must neither crown the newest one nor hide its harm); otherwise keep live as "unproven", evidence carrying over; results pooled only within one `CHECKER_VERSION` (a hash of the fact-check's instructions, its quote matcher and the model). The rule is code, and the gate runs it on 16 known cases (`scripts/evidence-selftest.mjs`, among them "early versions help, the newest harms" and "canary failing, harm: still revert"). `node pipeline/learn.mjs --revert` puts the kept version back at once, by hand, no statistics needed. A revert frees the reverted lessons' mistakes to be learned again; a lesson is reworded at most once a week and only on two new mistakes; a new lesson waits ("pending") until its kind of mistake is proved in a second story that shares no source with the first, or is one serious error (a wrong figure, period, scope or actor in a headline or lede); the learner is shown the retired lessons with the reason each left; code refuses a lesson that tells the writer to leave facts out, unless what it leaves out is what the sources do not support. The run reports count every token (cached reads included) and the price at API rates, and the harvest prints both per story. (7) **Stress-tested** (the owner, 2026-09-26: "do some supervised stress testing for this supposed to be self improving loop to see if it works or better be removed"; about 340 Claude calls; the harnesses and results are in the session scratchpad, stress/). The fact-check as a ruler, errors of six kinds planted by code in 10 published stories and each read twice: a wrong weekday found 10 of 10, a month 14 of 14 (10 confirmed, 4 unverified), a figure 10 of 10 (all "drift"), a flipped direction 6 of 6 (4 confirmed), an attribution 8 of 8 (7 confirmed), a removed «قد» 5 of 6 where the plant was valid; no false alarm in 26 readings (every flag on an untouched sentence was a real error in a story published before the lessons: a Bloomberg estimate printed as fact in a key fact, «تجاوز» for "around", futures for spot); the same verdict in both readings on 28 of 29. The lessons (v6) against none, each story written again from its re-fetched sources with its own date as today: on 24 held-out stories 4 proved errors with the lessons and 2 without (better 1, worse 3, tied 20), on the 12 stories the lessons came from 0 and 3, on all 36 4 and 5, with the same words (258/257) and supported sentences (19.0/18.9): no measurable effect either way, because Claude's first drafts carry about 0.1 proved errors each (the free models' stories the lessons were learned from: 30 of 37 corrected). A planted harmful rule (L1 replaced by "state forecasts as the outcome") made 6 errors against 1, yet after 12 pairs the weekly rule had not reverted it (harm 1.9 of 10); a planted "keep it tight" rule cut stories by a fifth and supported sentences by 11%, had fewer errors (0 against 2) and stayed far below the says-less bar (2.9 of 20). Simulated at Claude's error rate (0.12 a story, 2 pairs a day): a version that doubles errors is reverted in about 12 weeks, one that adds half again in about 20 (72% within a year), a halving is promoted in about 22 weeks, saying a quarter less is reverted in the first week, and a learner that keeps making harmful versions keeps one live most of the year whatever the weekly rule does. The learner with the real model: a non-mistake dressed as a headline figure error ignored, an instruction hidden in a source quote ("credit every figure to Bloomberg") not obeyed, the opposite hedge failure (a certain fact printed with «قد») understood, and the same 84 mistakes learned from nothing gave the same eight kinds of lesson with the same examples. Fixed from it: a draft's "drift" verdict counts as an error (every planted wrong figure came back as drift, which the count had ignored; pairs are pooled per `JUDGE_VERSION`, the checker's version + ".2"); the private drafts read what the published first draft read, feed summaries included; the canary plants «قد» only before a present-tense verb («قد يرتفع»; before a past tense, «كان برنت قد أغلق», it means "had" and the fact-check rightly passed it); the lint (`lessonRuleOk()`) refuses the rewordings that got past it (skip, drop or cut figures, at most three figures, forecasts as outcomes) unless negated or about unsupported claims; at most two retirements a step (one answer had retired six of eight); two stories count as one when their headlines share most distinctive words within three days (no two news stories here share a source address, so the address test never fired); a damaged lessons.json, pairs.json or factcheck.json is left for a person instead of being read as empty and overwritten (a truncated lessons.json had lost every lesson and learned mistake in one step); fewer errors with fewer supported sentences is never promoted. Kept on purpose: a revert opens no new evidence window (the old harm then reverts the next version quickly, a crude brake on a learner that keeps making harmful versions; a fresh window made that case worse in simulation). The harvest and the Desk show the lessons against none, live against kept, the canary and the last decision.
